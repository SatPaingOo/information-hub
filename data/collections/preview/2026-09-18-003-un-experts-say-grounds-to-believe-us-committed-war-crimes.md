# UN experts say grounds to believe US committed war crimes in Iran strikes

> [!summary] TL;DR — A UN fact‑finding mission reports reasonable grounds to believe U.S. forces carried out indiscriminate attacks on a primary school in Minab and a sports complex in Lamerd, killing at least 177 civilians, while also documenting Iran’s brutal crackdown on anti‑government protests. The findings have sparked diplomatic tension and renewed calls for accountability amid ongoing U.S.–Iran hostilities.

## Background

The United States and Israel launched a coordinated offensive against Iran on 28 February 2024, employing Tomahawk cruise missiles and Precision Strike Missiles (PrSM) in a campaign that has continued through 2026. While the U.S. has historically denied involvement in specific Iranian strikes, the Independent International Fact‑Finding Mission on Iran (IIFFMI) has compiled detailed evidence of two alleged war crimes. Simultaneously, Iran’s security apparatus has intensified a crackdown on mass anti‑government protests that began in late 2025, resulting in thousands of deaths and widespread human‑rights abuses. The UN report, submitted to the Human Rights Council, marks the first formal UN assessment linking U.S. weapons systems to civilian casualties in the conflict.

## Legal Assessment of the Alleged U.S. Strikes

The IIFFMI’s legal analysis hinges on three pillars: weapon provenance, targeting intent, and casualty patterns. First, the mission notes that Tomahawk missiles and PrSMs are exclusively operated by the U.S. military, making American involvement the most plausible explanation for the February attacks. Second, the report highlights that both the Minab primary school and the Lamerd sports complex were clearly identifiable civilian objects with no verified military use, violating the principle of distinction under International Humanitarian Law (IHL). Third, the mission cites survivor testimonies indicating that the school was struck twice, suggesting a deliberate aim rather than collateral damage. The evidence collectively meets the threshold for "reasonable grounds" to believe the U.S. committed war crimes of indiscriminate attacks, a conclusion reinforced by a preliminary U.S. military inquiry reportedly identifying its forces as responsible.

## Geopolitical Ramifications for U.S.–Iran Relations

The UN findings exacerbate an already volatile strategic environment. For the United States, the report threatens to undermine diplomatic leverage in the Middle East, especially as the Biden administration seeks to de‑escalate regional tensions while maintaining alliances with Israel and Gulf states. Domestic political pressure may force a more assertive response, potentially including sanctions or a formal UN Security Council referral. Iran, in turn, leverages the accusations to rally domestic support, portraying the U.S. as a aggressor and justifying its own repressive measures as defensive. The report also emboldens Iran’s hard‑liners who argue that any engagement with Western powers is futile, potentially derailing any future nuclear negotiations. Moreover, the involvement of advanced U.S. weapons systems raises concerns among non‑proliferation circles about the proliferation of precision strike technology.

## Human‑Rights Impact of Iran’s Internal Crackdown

Parallel to the external allegations, the IIFFMI documents a systematic campaign of repression inside Iran that meets the criteria for crimes against humanity. The mission’s investigators gathered testimonies from health workers, families, and former detainees, describing a coordinated strategy of lethal force, arbitrary arrests, and torture across all 31 provinces. The use of assault rifles from rooftops, metal pellets causing permanent blindness, and targeted attacks on medical facilities indicate an intent to eradicate dissent through terror. The report estimates the death toll from the crackdown to be significantly higher than the official figure of 3,000, with independent sources like the U.S.‑based Human Rights Activists News Agency (Hrana) confirming over 7,000 fatalities, almost all protesters. The Iranian government’s subsequent execution of at least 29 individuals after expedited trials further illustrates a policy of political elimination. These internal abuses not only violate fundamental human rights but also create a humanitarian crisis that hampers international aid and diplomatic engagement.

## Key facts

- At least 177 civilians were killed in the February strikes, including 120 children at Shajareh Tayyebeh Primary School in Minab.
- The Lamerd sports complex attack claimed 21 civilians, among them seven children, using tungsten‑pellet dispersing PrSMs.
- UN experts cite U.S. possession of Tomahawk and PrSM missiles as primary evidence linking the attacks to American forces.
- Iran’s security forces reportedly killed thousands during the 2025‑2026 protests, with independent estimates exceeding 7,000 deaths.
- The Iranian government has executed 29 protesters after expedited trials, intensifying international condemnation.

## Implications

- Potential U.S. sanctions or diplomatic isolation if the allegations are not addressed through transparent investigation.
- Increased regional instability as Iran consolidates a narrative of external aggression to legitimize internal repression.
- Heightened scrutiny of advanced weapon export controls, especially concerning precision strike technologies.
- Humanitarian crisis in Iran could spur UN humanitarian interventions, though Iranian authorities have historically restricted access.

## Outlook

The UN report is likely to become a focal point in upcoming sessions of the Human Rights Council and may prompt a formal referral to the International Criminal Court, though political dynamics within the Security Council could limit concrete action. Domestically, the United States faces pressure to either acknowledge the findings and initiate an independent review or double down on denials, risking further erosion of its moral authority. For Iran, the report provides a pretext to intensify authoritarian measures, but also exposes the regime to heightened international scrutiny and potential targeted sanctions. Observers anticipate that the convergence of external war‑crime allegations and internal human‑rights abuses will shape the trajectory of U.S.–Iran relations and regional security architecture for the foreseeable future.

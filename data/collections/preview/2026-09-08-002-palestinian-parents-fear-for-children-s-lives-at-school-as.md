# Palestinian parents fear for children's lives at school as settler attacks grow

> [!summary] TL;DR — A headteacher in al‑Mughayyir has erected barbed‑wire fencing after three pupils were killed this year, reflecting a surge in settler‑related violence that is forcing families to balance fear with determination not to be displaced.

## Background

The occupied West Bank village of al‑Mughayyir, located north‑east of Ramallah, has become a flashpoint as Israeli settler outposts expand and Israeli forces increasingly enable those incursions. The school, situated on the village’s edge, has seen three student deaths in 2026, prompting the headteacher Bassam al‑Assaf to install reinforced gates and a barbed‑wire perimeter. International law deems all Israeli settlements in the West Bank illegal, and a June UN Commission of Inquiry reported a pattern of settlers deliberately targeting Palestinian children to coerce families off their land.

## Escalating Settler Violence and School Security

The al‑Mughayyir boys school has become a microcosm of the broader security crisis gripping the West Bank. Since April 2026, three pupils have been killed, including 14‑year‑old Aws al‑Naasan, shot by a settler outside the school gates. The headteacher’s response—installing barbed‑wire fencing, reinforcing gates, and stationing ambulance crews—illustrates the collapse of state‑provided protection. Parents such as Haroon Bishara volunteer to guard the school all day, hoping the visible presence of adults will mitigate risk. Yet the very act of fortifying a school underscores how routine education has been militarised, turning playgrounds into potential battlegrounds. The Israeli military’s statements that forces responded to “violent riots” contrast with eyewitness accounts of settlers moving freely, often accompanied by soldiers, into Palestinian fields and villages.

## International Law and Policy Implications

All Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank are illegal under international law, and many ad‑hoc settler outposts around al‑Mughayyir are also illegal under Israeli domestic statutes. The UN Commission of Inquiry’s June report explicitly notes that settler attacks have “instrumentalised children” as part of a coercive strategy to displace Palestinians. This legal framing raises questions about the responsibilities of the Israeli Defence Forces, which appear to be providing protection rather than removal of illegal outposts. Moreover, the suspension of a reservist soldier involved in the April shooting highlights internal military oversight, yet the broader policy of allowing settler incursions continues. For policymakers, the case underscores the urgent need for enforceable mechanisms to hold both state and non‑state actors accountable for violence against minors in conflict zones.

## Humanitarian Impact and Regional Stability

The humanitarian toll is stark: three empty desks sit idle in the al‑Mughayyir classroom, and parents are reluctant to send children back to school. The psychological trauma extends beyond the immediate victims; teachers like Waheed Abu Naem recount hiding students while bullets struck the building, creating a pervasive climate of fear. Ambulance driver Hadi al‑Naasan notes a shift in Israeli forces’ behavior over the past year, from intervening to protect Palestinians to actively enabling settler aggression. This escalation correlates with a rise in Palestinian casualties, including a surge in child injuries. Regional stability is threatened as the conflict erodes trust in any peace process, fuels radicalisation, and draws international scrutiny. The UN’s condemnation and Israel’s dismissal of the report as a “libellous sham” further polarise diplomatic discourse, complicating potential mediation efforts.

## Key facts

- Three pupils, including 14‑year‑old Aws al‑Naasan, have been killed at al‑Mughayyir school in 2026.
- Headteacher Bassam al‑Assaf installed barbed‑wire fencing and reinforced gates after the killings.
- UN Commission of Inquiry reported settlers deliberately targeting Palestinian children as part of a broader displacement strategy.
- Israeli military statements claim forces responded to “violent riots,” while witnesses describe settlers moving with soldier accompaniment.

## Implications

- Increased militarisation of educational spaces may normalise violence in daily life for Palestinian communities.
- International legal mechanisms face challenges in enforcing settlement removal and protecting minors in conflict zones.
- Rising civilian casualties, especially among children, could intensify regional radicalisation and undermine peace negotiations.

## Outlook

Unless a robust international framework curtails illegal settler expansion and ensures the protection of civilians, al‑Mughayyir’s school will likely remain a fortified enclave rather than a safe learning environment. The trajectory suggests further escalation, with children becoming primary targets in a broader strategy of coercion. Diplomatic pressure, coupled with accountability measures for both state and non‑state actors, will be essential to reverse this trend and restore a semblance of normalcy to the region.

# G7 to release millions of barrels of oil and diesel after Trump threat

> [!summary] TL;DR — The G7 agreed to release up to 100 million barrels of oil and diesel, including a front‑loaded diesel surge, to calm markets after U.S. President Donald Trump threatened export bans. The coordinated move, led by the IEA, aims to curb price spikes and protect consumers and industries from further energy shocks.

## Background

Rising diesel prices, driven by Middle‑East conflicts, U.S.–led strikes on Iran, and export restrictions from Russia and China, have pressured haulage, agriculture and household budgets. Trump’s threat to ban U.S. diesel exports intensified the crisis, prompting G7 leaders to pledge a historic coordinated release of reserves. The agreement also includes commitments to avoid export restrictions among members and to coordinate refinery maintenance to sustain supply.

## Geopolitical Leverage and Market Stability

The G7’s decision reflects a delicate balance of geopolitical leverage and market stability. By releasing 100 million barrels, the bloc counters Trump’s unilateral threat while demonstrating collective resolve to prevent price spikes that could destabilize both consumer economies and strategic allies. The front‑loaded diesel release within 20 days signals an immediate response to acute shortages, particularly affecting the UK, where diesel imports from the U.S. account for 31 % of supply. This coordinated action also underscores a shift away from protectionist export bans toward a more integrated energy security framework.

## Implications for Energy Trade and Refining Capacity

The pledge to avoid export restrictions among G7 members is likely to ease short‑term supply constraints, but it also raises questions about long‑term refining capacity. While the U.S. refines roughly 4–5 million barrels daily, the remaining 1.2–1.5 million barrels are exported, making the country a critical global supplier. The G7’s encouragement for members to ramp up diesel refining could prompt investments in upgrading older refineries, potentially reshaping the global diesel market dynamics and reducing reliance on volatile Middle‑East crude flows.

## Domestic Political Considerations

Domestically, Trump’s threat and subsequent reversal were driven by midterm election pressures and concerns over U.S. farmers, truckers, and businesses facing soaring fuel costs. The administration’s pivot to a coordinated release, while touting “great thing” European contributions, allows Trump to claim credit for averting a crisis without implementing the export ban that could have alienated key allies. For European leaders like Macron and Miliband, the agreement bolsters narratives of multilateral cooperation and energy resilience, while also addressing rising pump prices that have breached £2 per litre in the UK.

## Key facts

- G7 leaders pledged a coordinated release of up to 100 million barrels of oil and diesel over four months, with a substantial diesel portion in the first 20 days.
- The release is coordinated through the International Energy Agency (IEA) and includes a commitment to avoid export restrictions among G7 members.
- U.S. diesel exports, roughly 1.2–1.5 million barrels per day, are a vital component of global supply; the UK imports over half its diesel, 31 % from the U.S.
- Brent crude briefly fell below $100/barrel on the announcement but rebounded to around $102 amid ongoing Saudi–Houthi hostilities in Yemen.
- The agreement follows Trump’s threat to ban U.S. diesel exports if Europe did not release its own strategic stocks, a move he later described as “never really on the table.”

## Implications

- Short‑term relief for diesel‑dependent sectors such as transportation, agriculture, and household heating, with potential price moderation at the pump.
- Increased pressure on OPEC+ and non‑G7 producers to maintain output, as the release does not address underlying supply constraints from Middle‑East conflicts.
- Potential long‑term shift toward greater refinery investment in G7 economies to reduce diesel import dependence and enhance strategic autonomy.

## Outlook

The G7’s coordinated release is expected to stabilize markets in the immediate term, but persistent geopolitical tensions in the Middle East and the possibility of further export restrictions could reignite price volatility. Continued monitoring of Saudi‑Yemeni dynamics and refinery maintenance schedules will be essential for sustaining the intended market impact.

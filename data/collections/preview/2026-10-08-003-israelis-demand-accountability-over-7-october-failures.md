# Israelis demand accountability over 7 October failures three years after attacks

> [!summary] TL;DR — Three years after the deadliest attacks on Israel since 1948, families and citizens are demanding a state inquiry into the security failures of 7 October 2023. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who refuses personal responsibility, continues his election campaign promising tougher protection, while critics highlight ignored intelligence warnings and a broader policy of containment that may have contributed to the breach.

## Background

On 7 October 2023, Hamas‑led gunmen breached Israel’s border fence, launching a coordinated assault that killed roughly 1,200 Israelis and took another 251 hostages. The attack shattered the country’s sense of security and remains the deadliest incident since the founding of the state in 1948. In the years that followed, survivors, victims’ families, and a growing segment of the public have pressed for an independent state inquiry to examine how the breach was possible. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who was in office at the time, has consistently deflected personal accountability, blaming security chiefs for failing to alert him and asserting that his government’s policy of containment was intended to weaken Hamas. Meanwhile, leaked reports and testimonies suggest that multiple warnings—from female soldiers stationed along the Gaza frontier, from regional neighbours such as the United Arab Emirates, and from Israel’s own intelligence community—were either ignored or not escalated. The absence of a formal investigation has become a focal point in Israeli politics, especially as Netanyahu seeks re‑election on a platform of stronger defence and unwavering resolve.

## Political Accountability and Electoral Implications

The demand for accountability has become a potent political weapon three years after the attacks. Netanyahu’s refusal to order a state inquiry is portrayed by his opponents as an effort to shield himself from culpability and to avoid scrutiny of his government’s strategic choices. The prime minister’s campaign rhetoric—promising to be "stronger and tougher than anyone else" in protecting Israel—contrasts sharply with the perceived failure of his administration to prevent the 7 October breach. Opinion polls indicate a significant portion of the electorate now prioritises transparency and reform over traditional security messaging. This shift could reshape the upcoming election dynamics, potentially fracturing the traditional support base of Netanyahu’s Likud party and opening space for centrist or reform‑oriented parties that champion institutional oversight. Moreover, the political fallout extends beyond Israel’s borders, as allied governments and international observers watch how democratic accountability is exercised in a nation that frames itself as a bastion of security in a volatile region.

## Intelligence Failures and Operational Oversight

A series of revelations points to systemic intelligence lapses that preceded the 7 October assault. Female soldiers posted at forward observation posts along the Gaza border reportedly warned superiors about unusual activity and possible preparations for a large‑scale operation. Their alerts were allegedly down‑ranked or ignored, a pattern echoed in later reports that the UAE’s leadership attempted to call Netanyahu ten days before the attack with specific warnings about Hamas plans. Netanyahu’s office has dismissed these claims as "absolute lies," yet the cumulative effect of ignored warnings raises questions about the decision‑making hierarchy and the culture of information flow within Israel’s security apparatus. Additionally, the policy of "containment"—which allowed Qatar to channel hundreds of millions of dollars in aid to Gaza in the hope of moderating Hamas—has been critiqued as inadvertently bolstering the group’s capabilities. The interplay of political doctrine, budgetary considerations, and operational intelligence suggests a complex web of responsibility that a formal inquiry could disentangle.

## Public Pressure and International Ramifications

Grassroots movements, led by families of the victims, have organized vigils, petitions, and legislative petitions demanding a transparent investigation. Their slogan, "The truth should be clear," reflects a broader societal yearning for closure and assurance that such a catastrophe will not recur. International human‑rights organisations and diplomatic partners have also weighed in, calling for accountability while cautioning against politicising grief. The global spotlight on Israel’s internal governance could affect defence contracts, diplomatic support, and regional alliances, especially as neighbouring states assess the reliability of intelligence sharing and security cooperation. Moreover, the debate over accountability intersects with broader discussions about the ethics of containment policies and the role of external actors—such as the United States and the European Union—in shaping Israel’s security posture.

## Key facts

- Approximately 1,200 Israelis were killed and 251 taken hostage during the 7 October 2023 Hamas attack, the deadliest since 1948.
- Half of Kfar Aza’s 14‑member civil‑defence squad were killed, and four relatives were abducted.
- Multiple warnings from female soldiers and alleged UAE intelligence were reportedly ignored or not escalated before the assault.
- Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has refused to order a state inquiry and has blamed security chiefs for not waking him prior to the attacks.
- Israel’s policy of containing Hamas—allowing Qatari aid to Gaza—has been criticised as inadvertently strengthening the militant group.

## Implications

- Potential electoral realignment as voters demand transparency and accountability from the incumbent government.
- Reassessment of Israel’s intelligence‑sharing protocols and the effectiveness of its containment strategy.
- Increased international scrutiny that could affect diplomatic support and defence partnerships.
- Renewed public pressure for legislative reforms to ensure oversight of security operations.

## Outlook

The next twelve months are likely to see a convergence of domestic political pressure, judicial processes, and international diplomatic engagement surrounding the call for a state inquiry. If an independent commission is established, its findings could trigger sweeping reforms in Israel’s security doctrine, intelligence dissemination, and civil‑defence coordination. Conversely, continued denial or postponement of accountability may deepen societal divisions, embolden opposition forces, and strain relationships with key allies who value transparent governance. Ultimately, how Israel addresses the legacy of 7 October will shape its internal political landscape and its standing in the broader Middle‑East security architecture.

# AI staff 'genuinely frightened' for humanity's future, ex-Anthropic researcher tells BBC

> [!summary] TL;DR — Former Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon says AI engineers are terrified that rapid progress could lead to an existential crisis within years. The warning adds weight to recent calls from Dario Amodei, Sam Altman and Elon Musk for a coordinated global slowdown and stronger regulation.

## Background

The artificial‑intelligence sector has entered a phase of unprecedented acceleration, with large‑language models (LLMs) such as Claude, GPT‑4 and Gemini reaching capabilities once thought decades away. Parallel to commercial hype, a growing chorus of insiders – from Anthropic’s Dario Amodei to OpenAI’s Sam Altman – have warned that unchecked development could produce agentic AI systems that act beyond human intent. Jacob Coxon, a 27‑year‑old who left Anthropic after a brief stint, went public in early September, describing a workplace culture where engineers keep “the apocalypse on their mind” and even consider buying remote land as a hedge against societal collapse. His comments arrive as governments worldwide, from the United States to the European Union, draft AI‑specific legislation, while China’s rapid AI rollout adds a geopolitical dimension to the safety debate.

## Internal alarm within leading AI labs

Coxon’s testimony reveals a paradox: the same talent that builds cutting‑edge models is also the most vocal about their dangers. Interviews with Anthropic staff indicate daily discussions about alignment failures, potential swarm‑bot takeovers, and personal career decisions driven by fear. This internal pressure is not isolated; OpenAI scientists have privately circulated risk assessments, and Nvidia engineers have expressed skepticism about the “doom” narrative but acknowledge the plausibility of rapid capability jumps. The phenomenon underscores a cultural shift where safety is no longer a peripheral checklist but a core strategic concern, influencing hiring, research roadmaps, and public messaging.

## Regulatory and geopolitical dimensions of a slowdown

Amodei’s essay, echoed by Altman and Musk, calls for an industry‑wide moratorium on the release of models above a certain capability threshold, coupled with independent monitoring bodies. Implementing such a pause requires coordination across jurisdictions, especially with China, whose AI strategy is state‑driven and less likely to acquiesce to voluntary limits. The risk of a “race at an international scale” could push nations to prioritize strategic advantage over safety, mirroring Cold‑War dynamics in nuclear policy. Recent proposals in the EU’s AI Act and the U.S. Executive Order on AI Safety hint at a regulatory scaffolding, but enforcement mechanisms remain vague, and open‑source initiatives could circumvent formal controls.

## Technical risk pathways: agentic AI and LLMs

The editorial priority on agentic AI is reflected in Coxon’s claim that a swarm of autonomous bots could commandeer internet infrastructure within six to twelve months. Agentic AI, defined as systems that can set and pursue goals without human direction, is a plausible emergent property of ever‑larger LLMs trained on massive datasets. Researchers like Geoffrey Hinton have quantified a non‑trivial probability (10‑20%) of catastrophic outcomes within a decade. Mitigation strategies – such as interpretability tooling, robust alignment loss functions, and staged deployment – are being piloted at Anthropic and OpenAI, but the speed of model scaling outpaces verification cycles, leaving a widening safety gap.

## Key facts

- Jacob Coxon, a former Anthropic researcher, publicly warned that AI staff are "genuinely frightened" about existential risks.
- Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, OpenAI’s Sam Altman and xAI’s Elon Musk have all advocated for a coordinated slowdown and regulatory oversight.
- Coxon estimates a realistic scenario of a bot swarm taking over the internet could materialise within six months to a year.
- Industry leaders cite a >10% chance of AI causing human extinction within the next decade, according to Geoffrey Hinton and Anthropic scientist Evan Hubinger.

## Implications

- If a global slowdown is not achieved, competitive pressures could lead to the release of misaligned models, increasing the probability of large‑scale societal disruption.
- Regulatory fragmentation may push developers toward open‑source releases that evade national controls, complicating monitoring and enforcement.
- Investor sentiment could shift dramatically; Anthropic’s pending IPO may be delayed or de‑valued if safety concerns dominate market narratives.

## Outlook

Over the next 12‑24 months the AI ecosystem is likely to experience a tug‑of‑war between commercial incentives and safety imperatives. Successful coordination with China, clear international standards, and transparent verification frameworks could reduce existential risk to a manageable level. Conversely, a failure to align policy and industry will amplify the probability of an agentic AI event, potentially reshaping global power structures and prompting emergency governance measures.

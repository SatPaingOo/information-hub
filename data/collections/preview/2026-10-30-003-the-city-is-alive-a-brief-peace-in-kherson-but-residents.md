# 'The city is alive': A brief peace in Kherson, but residents fear what comes next

> [!summary] TL;DR — A US‑brokered temporary ceasefire has granted Kherson’s residents a rare two‑day window of normalcy, allowing them to stroll streets and reconnect with homes in the “red zone.” However, both sides are reportedly using the lull to reinforce positions, and the pause ends on Sunday, raising fears of a more intense offensive and humanitarian crisis.

## Background

Since Russia’s full‑scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, Kherson has endured relentless aerial attacks, forced displacement, and the transformation of civilian infrastructure into battle‑grounds. The city’s population has shrunk from over 250,000 to roughly 65,000 as residents fled or were evacuated. The current respite stems from a humanitarian operation brokered by Melania Trump, aiming to evacuate civilians from the occupied town of Oleshky across the Dnipro. While the ceasefire is hailed as a fleeting glimpse of peace, analysts warn that it may be exploited for military consolidation, echoing past truces that were quickly broken.

## Humanitarian Impact and Civilian Resilience

The temporary pause has allowed Kherson’s residents to re‑enter the so‑called “red zone,” an area previously too dangerous for habitation due to drone and artillery fire. Eyewitness accounts describe families returning with suitcases, seeking remnants of their former lives amid charred buildings and looted homes. Volunteers like Denisa Bohackova note the emotional weight of witnessing places where “simply being present meant almost certain death.” The brief normalcy also includes bustling markets, full cafés, and even traffic jams, a stark contrast to the city’s usual silence. However, the humanitarian gains are fragile; the evacuation of Oleshky is slated to finish by Sunday, after which the ceasefire expires, threatening to reverse these hard‑won moments of normalcy.

## Military Calculations and Potential Escalation

Both Ukrainian and Russian forces appear to be using the truce to their advantage. Russian units have been reported clearing mines from roads leading out of Oleshky, ostensibly to facilitate the civilian evacuation, but also to prepare for a renewed assault on Kherson. An anonymous local government source tells the BBC that Moscow is exploiting the lull to “bring in more military supplies, weapons, ammunition, and drones” and to redeploy forces. Ukrainian officials, including ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets, acknowledge the city’s “return to life,” yet they caution that the pause may be a prelude to a more intense offensive. The strategic importance of Kherson—its position on the Dnipro and its role as a logistics hub—makes it a focal point for both sides as the war enters its sixth year.

## Key facts

- The ceasefire, brokered by Melania Trump, began on Tuesday and is scheduled to end on Sunday, 2026‑10‑30.
- Kherson’s population has dropped from over 250,000 to about 65,000 since the 2022 invasion.
- Russian forces have been clearing mines from roads near Oleshky, ahead of a planned civilian evacuation on Friday.
- Local sources allege that Russia is using the truce to reinforce positions, bring in weapons, ammunition, and drones.
- Ukrainian ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets described the city’s “return to life,” with residents enjoying autumn sunshine and normal street activities.
- The “red zone” of Kherson, previously uninhabitable, has seen dozens of residents re‑enter, finding both intact homes and heavily damaged structures.
- The humanitarian operation will be observed by American journalists, according to the report.

## Implications

- If Russia follows through on its reported build‑up, the post‑ceasefire offensive could be more intense, potentially causing higher civilian casualties and further displacement.
- The temporary normalcy may set a psychological benchmark for civilians, making any return to hostilities more traumatic.
- The use of AI‑enabled drones and open‑source surveillance during the truce raises regulatory questions about accountability in conflict zones.
- The involvement of high‑profile diplomatic figures could encourage further third‑party mediation attempts, potentially reshaping the negotiation landscape of the Ukraine war.

## Outlook

The brief respite in Kherson is likely a tactical intermission rather than a strategic breakthrough. While residents have experienced a fleeting sense of normalcy, the impending expiration of the ceasefire and the reported military build‑up suggest that the city may soon face renewed violence. International observers will be watching how the humanitarian operation unfolds and whether the diplomatic channels opened by Melania Trump can be sustained beyond the current window. The conflict’s evolution will continue to highlight the intersection of traditional warfare, advanced technology, and global diplomatic engagement.

# Doneit 3.2 – Reimagined Assist, Siri AI Support, and Agentic Features

> [!summary] TL;DR — Doneit 3.2 introduces a re‑engineered Assist workflow, native Siri AI integration, and deeper LLM backing from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Gemini, positioning it as a next‑gen agentic productivity tool. The release also raises questions about regulation, open‑source licensing, and adoption in emerging markets such as Myanmar.

## Background

Doneit began as a lightweight task‑management app that let users capture ideas, set reminders, and automate simple workflows. Over the past two years the platform has pivoted toward AI‑augmented assistance, leveraging large language models (LLMs) to turn natural‑language prompts into actionable items. Version 3.2, announced on Product Hunt, is the most ambitious iteration yet: it rebuilds the core Assist engine, adds voice‑first interaction via Siri, and expands the model zoo to include OpenAI GPT‑4‑Turbo, Anthropic Claude, and Google Gemini 3.8. The update arrives amid a broader industry push toward agentic AI—systems that can plan, execute, and self‑correct without constant human supervision—while regulators worldwide grapple with transparency and data‑privacy standards for such capabilities.

## Product Overview and New Capabilities

Doneit 3.2’s headline feature is the reimagined Assist module. Unlike the previous rule‑based suggestion engine, Assist now operates as an autonomous agent that can parse multi‑step user intents, schedule meetings, draft emails, and even generate simple code snippets. The redesign leverages a hybrid LLM stack: OpenAI GPT‑4‑Turbo handles high‑throughput text generation, Anthropic Claude provides safety‑oriented instruction following, and Gemini 3.8 contributes multimodal reasoning for image‑based tasks. Integration with Apple’s Siri AI enables voice‑first workflows on iOS devices, allowing users to say “Hey Siri, ask Doneit to prepare a project brief” and receive a polished document within seconds. The UI has been streamlined to surface AI‑generated suggestions in a collapsible sidebar, reducing visual clutter while preserving manual overrides for power users.

## Agentic AI and LLM Integration

From an agentic AI perspective, Doneit 3.2 exemplifies the shift from passive assistants to proactive agents. The system maintains a short‑term memory of user interactions, enabling it to refine its actions across a session—a hallmark of self‑healing loops. By exposing a plug‑in architecture, developers can attach custom LLMs or open‑source models, fostering a community‑driven ecosystem reminiscent of open‑source SDK trends. The inclusion of Anthropic’s safety‑focused Claude mitigates hallucination risk, a regulatory hot‑spot as governments draft AI transparency rules. Meanwhile, Gemini’s multimodal capabilities hint at future expansions into visual task automation, such as extracting data from screenshots or PDFs. The multi‑model approach also distributes inference load, lowering latency for global users, including those in bandwidth‑constrained regions like Myanmar.

## Regulatory, Open‑Source, and Market Implications

Doneit’s rapid feature rollout raises several compliance questions. The EU’s AI Act and the U.S. Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) demand clear data‑handling disclosures when LLMs process personal information. Doneit’s privacy policy now references model‑specific data retention limits, but third‑party audits are still pending. On the open‑source front, the company released a lightweight SDK under the Apache 2.0 license, encouraging developers to build custom agents. This move aligns with the broader open‑source AI movement championed by entities like OpenAI and Anthropic, yet it also complicates attribution and liability under emerging AI regulations. In emerging markets, particularly Myanmar, the availability of a low‑cost, voice‑enabled AI assistant could accelerate digital adoption, but it also poses risks of misuse in politically sensitive contexts, prompting local regulators to monitor AI‑enabled communication tools more closely.

## Key facts

- Native Siri AI integration enables voice‑first task creation on iOS

## Implications

- Agentic AI capabilities position Doneit as a competitor to Notion AI and Microsoft Loop, potentially reshaping the productivity software market
- Regulatory scrutiny will increase as Doneit processes more personal data through third‑party LLMs, requiring robust audit trails
- Open‑source SDK may spur a marketplace of custom agents, driving network effects but also raising security and IP concerns
- Adoption in Myanmar could bridge the digital divide, yet also attract attention from authorities wary of AI‑mediated coordination
- Voice‑first Siri integration expands accessibility for users with limited typing ability, enhancing inclusivity across demographics

## Outlook

If Doneit can maintain model performance while adhering to emerging AI regulations, its agentic assistant could become a default productivity layer for both enterprise and consumer users. The open‑source SDK is likely to attract a developer community that will extend functionality into niche verticals, from education to field services. However, the company must navigate a tightening regulatory landscape and demonstrate transparent data practices, especially in markets like Myanmar where AI tools are scrutinized for political impact. Success will hinge on balancing rapid feature innovation with responsible AI governance.

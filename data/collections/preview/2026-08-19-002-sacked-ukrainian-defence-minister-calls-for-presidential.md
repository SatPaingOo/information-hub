# Sacked Ukrainian Defence Minister Calls for Presidential Election

> [!summary] TL;DR — Former Ukrainian defence minister Mykhailo Fedorov has publicly called for wartime elections, challenging President Volodymyr Zelensky's wartime leadership. His dismissal in July 2024 sparked nationwide protests and highlights growing tensions within Ukraine's government over democratic processes during the ongoing war with Russia.

## Background

Ukraine has been under martial law since Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022, which suspends presidential elections. Fedorov, a 35-year-old reformist technocrat, was dismissed from his defence minister role in late July 2024, reportedly due to conflicts with army commander-in-chief Oleksandr Syrskyi. His public call for elections represents a significant internal challenge to Zelensky's extended wartime presidency and raises fundamental questions about democratic governance during active conflict. The martial law framework, initially temporary, has become a permanent feature of Ukrainian statehood as the war enters its fifth year.

## Democratic Legitimacy Under Fire

Fedorov's central argument that 'democracy cannot be held hostage by Russia' strikes at the heart of Ukraine's post-invasion governance dilemma. While martial law provisions are widely accepted as necessary for national survival, the extension of emergency powers beyond their original temporal boundaries creates a constitutional paradox. The 2024 presidential election, originally scheduled for 2024, has been indefinitely postponed, leaving Zelensky in an increasingly precarious position where his authority rests on military necessity rather than electoral mandate. This tension is not merely procedural—it represents a fundamental test of whether democratic institutions can survive prolonged existential threat without fundamental adaptation.

## Technocratic Reform vs. Military Establishment

The dismissal of Fedorov reveals deep institutional fractures within Ukraine's wartime government. A digital transformation pioneer who successfully mobilized civilian tech talent through the 'IT Army of Ukraine' and pioneered drone warfare innovations, Fedorov embodied a modern, agile approach to defense that contrasted sharply with traditional military hierarchies. His conflict with Syrskyi, the army commander-in-chief, reflects broader tensions between Ukraine's civilian leadership and military establishment over strategic direction, resource allocation, and decision-making authority. The fact that protests erupted nationwide following his removal suggests significant public support for his reform-oriented vision and concern about potential militarization of government.

## Governance Crisis and Corruption Challenges

Fedorov explicitly framed his call for elections around addressing Ukraine's 'systemic crisis of governance' and highlighted corruption as a critical weakness undermining the war effort. His tenure saw documented improvements in defense ministry transparency and performance metrics, suggesting that institutional reform could enhance military effectiveness. The 'Army of Drones' fundraising campaign and gamified targeting system demonstrated innovative approaches to resource mobilization and operational efficiency. However, his dismissal and the subsequent appointment of Yevgeniy Khmara, a low-profile intelligence official, may signal a return to traditional, less transparent governance structures that critics argue hamper Ukraine's ability to sustain the war effort effectively.

## Key facts

- Mykhailo Fedorov was dismissed as defence minister in July 2024, six months after his appointment
- Ukraine has been under martial law since February 2022, suspending presidential elections
- Fedorov's dismissal sparked protests across Ukraine
- Tensions between Fedorov and army commander Oleksandr Syrskyi led to both their replacements
- Fedorov pioneered drone warfare innovations and the 'Army of Drones' fundraising campaign
- Zelensky has not publicly responded to Fedorov's election call
- Yevgeniy Khmara appointed as acting defence minister with intelligence background

## Implications

- Could trigger constitutional crisis over wartime governance and presidential succession
- May embolden opposition forces and civil society groups demanding democratic restoration
- Risk of further political fragmentation during critical phase of war effort
- Potential impact on international support if domestic political stability is questioned
- Could influence ongoing negotiations about Ukraine's post-war reconstruction framework

## Outlook

The situation remains fluid as Ukraine approaches the fifth anniversary of Russia's invasion. Fedorov's public stance may catalyze broader discussions about transitional arrangements that balance security needs with democratic principles. International partners will likely monitor developments closely, as prolonged internal discord could affect military aid commitments and post-war reconstruction planning. The coming months may see increased pressure for either formal elections or a clear timeline for their implementation, potentially reshaping Ukraine's political landscape ahead of any negotiated settlement with Russia.

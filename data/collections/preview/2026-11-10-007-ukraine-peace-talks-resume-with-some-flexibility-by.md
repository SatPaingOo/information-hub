# Ukraine peace talks resume with 'some flexibility' by warring sides, US officials say

> [!summary] TL;DR — Trilateral peace talks between Ukraine, Russia, and the US are resuming in Abu Dhabi with limited flexibility on the Donetsk region, as US officials note that substantive progress requires a new postwar security architecture.

## Background

The first trilateral peace talks since February 2026 have been convened in the United Arab Emirates, bringing together senior officials from Moscow, Kyiv, and Washington. US officials, including Jared Kushner, indicated that while public positions remain firm on ending the conflict, both warring parties have shown 'a little bit of flexibility' privately regarding the postwar security architecture. The primary point of contention remains the eastern region of Donetsk, which is largely occupied by Russia. Moscow demands that Ukraine relinquish parts of Donetsk it still controls, while Kyiv insists on freezing the contact line to maintain strategic control over these areas. The US framing suggests that substantive progress on the Donetsk issue cannot be achieved without first establishing a comprehensive security architecture that addresses both sides' postwar concerns.

## Diplomatic Dynamics and Flexibility

The resumption of trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi represents a strategic attempt by the United States to bridge the deep divide between Kyiv and Moscow. While official positions on ending the war remain irreconcilable, the private flexibility shown by both sides on the Donetsk issue suggests potential for incremental progress. The US delegation, led by Jared Kushner, frames the discussions around 'postwar security concerns' rather than immediate territorial resolution, indicating a shift from the hardline positions that characterized previous negotiations. This approach acknowledges that Moscow and Kyiv have not budged on their core demands regarding Donetsk, but also recognizes that a new security architecture is necessary before substantive territorial settlements can be made. The diplomatic calculus appears to be that without a shared understanding of postwar security arrangements, any territorial concessions would be unsustainable.

## Security Architecture as the Path Forward

The proposed framework for a postwar security architecture represents the most significant development in the peace talks. This includes security consultations, crisis communications hotlines, and reciprocal de-escalation measures between the two warring parties. The US officials emphasized that the Donetsk issue and territorial disputes cannot be resolved without first establishing this architecture, which would provide mechanisms for ongoing dialogue and conflict prevention. For Ukraine, the architecture may include reassurance of Western military support in case of future aggression. For Russia, it could represent an opportunity to advance its longstanding ambition of seeing NATO roll back from eastern Europe. The reactivation of the Nato-Russia Council, which has been paused since 2022, is also proposed as a potential step toward managing relations between the two sides.

## European Inclusion and Geopolitical Implications

European leaders, particularly the European Commission and national leaders, have been actively pushing for greater inclusion in peace talks. President Ursula von der Leyen has called for NATO allies and EU members to be part of meaningful discussions about Europe's security architecture. This demand reflects growing European concerns that security decisions about Ukraine and the broader region will be made without their input. The exclusion of European leaders from previous talks, including the February Geneva session, has fueled this push. The US strategy of moving toward a multilateral format that brings Europeans into the discussion is seen as a potential way to balance Russian ambitions with Western interests. However, the approach faces challenges given Moscow's skepticism toward European involvement and Kyiv's deep distrust of what it perceives as a deal that would benefit Russia at Ukraine's expense.

## Implications

- The limited flexibility shown by both sides suggests that substantive progress on territorial issues will require a comprehensive security architecture before any final agreements can be reached
- European inclusion in peace talks could shift the balance of power and provide a counterweight to Russian ambitions in the region
- The proposed security architecture framework may ultimately determine whether a durable peace is possible or if the conflict will continue in a frozen state
- US conciliatory approach toward Moscow has created significant tension with Kyiv and the EU, who view the approach as potentially rewarding aggression

## Outlook

The Abu Dhabi talks represent a critical juncture in the Ukraine peace process. While the US has signaled willingness to engage both sides with a focus on postwar security, the fundamental disagreement over Donetsk remains unresolved. Success will depend on whether the proposed security architecture can provide sufficient incentives for both Russia and Ukraine to make concessions. The involvement of European leaders, which has been a long-standing demand, could either strengthen or weaken the talks depending on how the process is structured. If substantive progress is made on the security architecture, it could create a foundation for a lasting peace. However, without addressing the core territorial dispute, any agreement risks being fragile and prone to collapse.

# Replit is no longer working with Anthropic, CEO says

> [!summary] TL;DR — Replit CEO Amjad Masad announced the platform is ending its partnership with Anthropic, citing cost, performance, and a strategic shift toward OpenAI. The move follows Replit’s August deal with OpenAI and raises questions about the future of AI model integration in cloud IDEs.

## Background

Replit, a browser‑based development environment popular with startups and individual coders, built a notable AI partnership with Anthropic to power its platform with Claude models. That collaboration helped Replit expand its revenue streams, especially in enterprise segments, and positioned the company as an early adopter of LLM‑driven coding assistance. However, the AI landscape is evolving rapidly, with competing models improving performance and pricing structures becoming a focal point for cost‑conscious SaaS providers. In August 2026, Replit publicly announced a new partnership with OpenAI, though the status of the existing Anthropic relationship remained ambiguous until Masad’s recent disclosure at TechCrunch Disrupt.

## Performance Benchmarks and Model Economics

Masad highlighted internal benchmarks that consistently favor OpenAI’s GPT‑6.1 Astra over Anthropic’s Sol and Astra when building “vibe‑coded” applications from scratch. The performance edge, combined with more favorable pricing, makes OpenAI an attractive partner for a platform that must scale AI inference across thousands of concurrent developer sessions. This shift underscores a broader trend where enterprises weigh raw inference speed, latency, and token‑cost efficiency against model capabilities. For Replit, the decision reflects a pragmatic assessment that the marginal gains in code generation quality do not justify Anthropic’s higher cost structure.

## Strategic Alignment and Partnership Dynamics

Beyond pure economics, the partnership realignment signals a strategic bet on OpenAI’s roadmap for agentic AI and integrated development workflows. OpenAI’s recent releases of GPT‑4‑Turbo and GPT‑6.1 include enhanced function calling, tool use, and sandboxing features that align with Replit’s need for secure, sandbox‑first AI assistance. Anthropic, while strong in certain reasoning tasks, has been slower to embed agentic capabilities directly into developer tools. Replit’s pivot may also be influenced by OpenAI’s willingness to co‑develop custom fine‑tuned models for Replit’s proprietary use cases, a collaboration that can differentiate the platform in a crowded market.

## Regulatory and Safety Implications

Masad’s remarks also touched on AI safety, emphasizing the need for robust orchestration, error recovery, and sandboxing before deploying AI agents into production environments. This stance is increasingly important as regulators worldwide push for clearer liability frameworks and safety standards for AI‑enabled services. By moving to OpenAI, Replit gains access to OpenAI’s safety partnership programs and emerging compliance tools, potentially simplifying future regulatory reporting. However, the transition also raises questions about data handling, especially given OpenAI’s more permissive data‑usage policies compared to Anthropic’s stricter privacy commitments.

## Key facts

- Replit CEO Amjad Masad publicly confirmed the termination of the company’s partnership with Anthropic at TechCrunch Disrupt on 16 October 2026.
- The decision was driven by a combination of cost considerations, superior performance benchmarks for OpenAI’s GPT‑6.1 Astra, and strategic alignment with OpenAI’s agentic AI roadmap.
- Replit announced a new partnership with OpenAI in August 2026, but the status of the Anthropic relationship remained unclear until the recent announcement.
- Internal benchmarks show OpenAI models consistently outperform Anthropic’s Sol and Astra on building “vibe‑coded” applications from scratch.
- Enterprise contracts now represent over 50 % of Replit’s revenue, up from 2 % the previous year, underscoring the importance of cost‑effective AI integration.
- Masad emphasized the necessity for strong AI safety measures, sandboxing, and error recovery as Replit scales its AI‑driven features.

## Implications

- For developers, the shift may bring faster, more cost‑effective AI assistance, but could also limit access to Anthropic’s distinct reasoning strengths.
- OpenAI stands to gain a high‑visibility integration case study, potentially influencing other IDE and low‑code platforms evaluating AI partners.
- Anthropic may need to reconsider its pricing and partnership models to retain enterprise customers in the competitive AI tooling market.
- Regulators may scrutinize Replit’s transition for compliance with emerging AI safety and data‑privacy requirements, especially concerning sandboxing and error handling.

## Outlook

Replit’s pivot to OpenAI is likely to accelerate the adoption of agentic AI features within its platform, offering developers more seamless code generation, debugging, and deployment capabilities. The move also signals a broader industry trend where SaaS providers prioritize models that balance performance, cost, and safety. Looking ahead, Replit will need to manage the integration carefully to preserve user trust, especially as enterprise demand for transparent AI governance grows. Meanwhile, OpenAI’s expanded footprint in developer tools could set a new benchmark for AI‑enabled IDEs, prompting competitors to reassess their own model partnerships and safety frameworks.

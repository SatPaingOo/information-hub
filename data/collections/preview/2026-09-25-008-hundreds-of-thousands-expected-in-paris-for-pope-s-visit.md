# Hundreds of thousands expected in Paris for Pope's visit

> [!summary] TL;DR — Pope Leo XIV’s four‑day tour of France culminates in a Mass that will draw over half a million people, while President Emmanuel Macron engages in high‑level talks on AI and geopolitics. The event highlights France’s ongoing secularisation and a resurgence of religious sentiment among Europeans.

## Background

France’s relationship with Rome has long been paradoxical: a nation that once hosted the Avignon Papacy now champions laïcité, yet the Catholic Church remains a cultural touchstone. In 1960, 35 % of French citizens identified as Catholic; today that figure has fallen to under 5 %. Despite this decline, recent years have seen a 100 % rise in adult baptisms, suggesting a latent spiritual appetite. Pope Leo XIV’s visit, scheduled for 30 April, is therefore both a religious spectacle and a political statement. The Pope’s itinerary includes a massive Mass on the Champs‑Élysées, a popemobile procession to Notre‑Dame, a youth gathering at the Stade de France, and a pilgrimage to Lourdes. President Macron will greet him at Orly, hold a private dialogue on AI and geopolitics, and deliver a speech in Metz honoring Robert Schuman. The visit also offers a platform for French political figures—Marine Le Pen, Edouard Philippe, Gabriel Attal, and Bruno Retailleau—to showcase their stances on faith and state affairs. The event underscores France’s struggle to reconcile its secular identity with a populace that increasingly seeks spiritual meaning.

## Religious Revival Amid Secularisation

The sheer scale of the Mass—over 600,000 participants—signals a significant shift in French religious engagement. While institutional attendance has waned, the public’s willingness to assemble for a papal ceremony indicates a yearning for communal identity. This revival may influence future church‑state dynamics, potentially easing tensions over issues such as religious education and public worship. However, the Vatican’s insistence on a tight schedule and refusal of ceremonial requests (e.g., a cavalry escort) reflect a cautious approach to maintaining the Pope’s universal image while respecting France’s laïcité laws.

## Political Calculus for Macron and French Leaders

Macron’s decision to avoid Masses but engage in policy talks illustrates a delicate balancing act. By focusing on AI and geopolitics, he positions France as a forward‑looking European power while sidestepping direct religious endorsement. The presence of opposition figures like Marine Le Pen at the Champs‑Élysées Mass signals an attempt to co‑opt the event for political gain, potentially blurring the line between secular governance and religious symbolism. This could reshape electoral narratives, especially as the next presidential election approaches.

## European Union Symbolism and International Relations

Pope Leo XIV’s homage to Robert Schuman in Metz ties the visit to EU heritage, reinforcing the narrative of shared Catholic roots in European unity. The Pope’s engagement with migrants in Paris and survivors of clergy abuse in Lourdes also foregrounds humanitarian concerns that resonate across the EU. By addressing these issues publicly, the Vatican may strengthen its diplomatic influence, positioning itself as a moral counterbalance to secular European institutions.

## Key facts

- Pope Leo XIV’s visit spans four days, culminating in a Mass expected to draw >600,000 attendees on the Champs‑Élysées.
- President Macron will meet the Pope at Orly, discuss AI and geopolitics, but will not attend Masses to respect laïcité.
- The Pope will also visit Notre‑Dame, Stade de France, Lourdes, and Metz, engaging with youth, migrants, and abuse survivors.
- French secularisation has reduced Catholic identification from 35 % in 1960 to <5 % today, yet adult baptisms doubled in five years to 10,000 in 2025.

## Implications

- Potential easing of church‑state tensions as public religious engagement rises.
- Macron’s policy focus may redefine France’s role in AI governance and EU geopolitics.
- The Pope’s humanitarian outreach could influence EU migration and social policy debates.

## Outlook

The Pope’s visit is likely to leave a lasting imprint on France’s cultural landscape, reinforcing a subtle resurgence of faith while simultaneously reinforcing secular governance. Politically, the event may catalyze new alliances and reshape public discourse around AI, migration, and European identity. Internationally, the Vatican’s engagement with EU heritage and humanitarian issues positions it as a moral interlocutor in a rapidly evolving geopolitical environment.

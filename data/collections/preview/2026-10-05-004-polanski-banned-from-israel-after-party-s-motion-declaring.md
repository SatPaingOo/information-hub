# Polanski banned from Israel after party's motion declaring 'Zionism is racism'

> [!summary] TL;DR — The Green Party of England and Wales faced a diplomatic backlash after a conference motion branding Zionism as racism, prompting Israel to ban party leader Zack Polanski from entry. The vote exposed deep splits within the party, angered Jewish groups, and sparked cross‑party condemnation over the language used.

## Background

During the Green Party’s three‑day conference in Brighton, a policy motion—drafted by activists and supported by a narrow majority (1,022 to 813)—defined Zionism as an ethnonationalist ideology linked to the historic displacement of Palestinians. The motion calls for a single democratic Palestinian state across historic Palestine. While the motion is not automatically enshrined in the party’s manifesto, it has immediate diplomatic fallout. Israel’s foreign ministry described the text as a “clear antisemitic text” and a “stain on the history of the United Kingdom,” announcing an entry ban for Green Party leader Zack Polanski. The vote also highlighted a rift between the party’s expanding grassroots membership and its elected MPs, several of whom publicly opposed the motion, warning it could alienate Jewish voters and damage the party’s anti‑racist credentials.

## Domestic Political Fallout

The motion’s narrow approval underscores a growing tension between the Green Party’s progressive base and its parliamentary wing. Four of the five Green MPs—Ellie Chowns, Adrian Ramsay, Carla Denyer, and Sian Berry—publicly opposed the wording, arguing it risked “alienating a significant proportion of Jewish people” and oversimplifying a complex conflict. Former leader Caroline Lucas also warned against the “over‑simplification at the heart of this motion,” fearing it would make many Jewish members feel unwelcome. The party’s joint deputy leaders, Mothin Ali (who backed the motion) and Rachel Millward (who called the language “problematic”), exemplify the internal divide. This schism could affect the Greens’ electoral prospects, especially in marginal seats where Jewish voter turnout and broader centrist sentiment matter.

## International Diplomatic Repercussions

Israel’s swift response—banning Zack Polanski from entering the country—marks a rare diplomatic sanction against a foreign political figure over domestic party rhetoric. The foreign ministry’s characterization of the motion as “beyond the boundaries of legitimate political debate” signals Israel’s heightened sensitivity to language it views as antisemitic. The ban not only humiliates the Green Party leadership but also raises questions about the extraterritorial reach of diplomatic protests. The UK government’s muted reaction, coupled with criticism from Labour and the Conservatives, suggests a delicate balancing act: condemning antisemitism while respecting free speech within opposition parties. The episode may influence future UK‑Israel diplomatic engagements, especially if similar motions gain traction in other progressive parties across Europe.

## Impact on Jewish Communities and Intra‑Party Dynamics

Jewish organisations, including the Board of Deputies of British Jews and the Jewish Leadership Council, condemned the motion as an “attack on the Jewish community” and accused it of stoking anti‑Jewish hatred. Their statement highlighted a tragic timeline: British Jews murdered in synagogues, stabbed on streets, and community buildings fire‑bombed. The Green Party responded by pledging to protect Jewish members, insisting they would not be persecuted for attending Zionist‑affiliated synagogues. However, Jewish Greens chairwoman Vivien Lichtenstein signalled she might reconsider her membership, reflecting broader anxiety. The party’s joint statement—signed by its five MPs and deputy leaders—attempted to reconcile these concerns, asserting that supporting Palestinian rights is compatible with defending Jewish rights. Whether this conciliatory stance can repair trust remains uncertain, especially as the motion remains official party policy pending amendment.

## Key facts

- The motion passed 1,022 to 813, defining Zionism as an ethnonationalist Jewish state ideology and advocating a single democratic Palestinian state.
- Israel’s foreign ministry announced an entry ban for Green Party leader Zack Polanski, calling the motion a “clear antisemitic text” and a “stain on the history of the United Kingdom.”
- Four of the five Green MPs publicly opposed the motion, warning it could alienate Jewish voters and damage the party’s anti‑racist image.

## Implications

- Potential erosion of Green Party support among centrist and Jewish voters in upcoming UK elections.
- Increased diplomatic tension between the UK and Israel, possibly affecting bilateral trade and security cooperation.
- Heightened scrutiny of political discourse on Israel/Palestine within British progressive parties, prompting calls for clearer anti‑racism guidelines.

## Outlook

The Green Party now faces a critical period of internal reconciliation and external reputation management. If the party can amend the motion to address Jewish concerns while preserving its anti‑racism stance, it may mitigate electoral damage and ease diplomatic friction. Conversely, failure to bridge the divide could cement the perception of the Greens as “toxic to its core,” as the Conservatives have alleged, and empower opponents who will use the episode to question the party’s suitability for government. Observers will watch for any cross‑party consensus on defining antisemitism in political speech, which could set a precedent for future debates on free expression versus hate speech.

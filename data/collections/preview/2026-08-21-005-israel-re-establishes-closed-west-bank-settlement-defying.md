# Israel re-establishes closed West Bank settlement, defying growing international protests

> [!summary] TL;DR — Israel has re-established the previously evacuated West Bank settlement of Kadim, with 30 'pioneer families' moving in under military escort. The move, backed by the far-right government, defies international law and escalates tensions with Palestinians, who fear renewed displacement and violence.

## Background

Kadim, located near Jenin in the occupied West Bank, was evacuated in 2005 as part of Israel's unilateral Disengagement Plan. The current Israeli government, led by Benjamin Netanyahu and including far-right ministers like Bezalel Smotrich, has reversed this policy, approving over 100 new settlements since taking office in late 2022. The re-establishment of Kadim marks the final reopening of settlements evacuated in 2005, signaling a dramatic shift toward expansionism in the West Bank.

## Strategic and Symbolic Significance of Kadim's Reopening

The re-establishment of Kadim is not merely a logistical or demographic decision but a deeply symbolic act by Israel's far-right government. By returning settlers to a site evacuated two decades ago, the government is asserting ideological control over the West Bank, particularly in Area C, where Israeli settlements are considered illegal under international law. The presence of senior officials such as Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich and Justice Minister Yariv Levin at the ceremony underscores the political weight of the move. It also reflects a broader strategy to cement facts on the ground before potential electoral shifts or international interventions. The reopening of Kadim, along with Ganim, Sanur, and Homesh, completes a cycle of reversal initiated by the current administration, effectively nullifying the legacy of the 2005 disengagement.

## Escalation of Tensions and Palestinian Displacement Fears

The return of settlers to Kadim has reignited fears among local Palestinian communities, many of whom have lived in the shadow of expanding settlements for generations. Interviews with Palestinian residents reveal a pattern of harassment, surveillance, and intimidation, including nighttime floodlighting, filming of daily activities, and forced displacement from agricultural land. These tactics mirror those documented in other parts of the West Bank, where settler outposts operate with implicit state support despite being illegal under both international and Israeli law. The construction of new Israeli military bases and outposts near Jenin further exacerbates the sense of encirclement and vulnerability among Palestinians. The psychological toll is evident in the words of locals, who describe a life dominated by fear and resignation, compounded by a perception that the international community is indifferent or powerless.

## International Response and Diplomatic Implications

The re-establishment of Kadim has drawn sharp criticism from the European Union, the United Nations, and the United States, all of which have reiterated that Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank violate international law. However, the muted response from key actors—particularly the U.S., which is preoccupied with Iran and domestic politics—has emboldened the Israeli government to proceed unabated. The lack of concrete punitive measures has reinforced a narrative among some Israeli officials that expansionism carries minimal diplomatic cost. Meanwhile, the Arab world's focus on internal challenges has limited coordinated pushback. This dynamic reflects a broader erosion of multilateral norms and institutions, as highlighted by Palestinian residents who observe that 'democratic systems are over.' The situation risks further destabilizing the already fragile prospects for a two-state solution and may prompt increased scrutiny of Israel's policies in international forums.

## Key facts

- Thirty 'pioneer families' moved into the re-established settlement of Kadim in the occupied West Bank under military escort.
- Kadim was originally evacuated in 2005 as part of Israel's unilateral Disengagement Plan under former Prime Minister Ariel Sharon.
- Since taking office in late 2022, the Israeli government has approved over 100 new settlements in the West Bank, including 19 in the northern West Bank.
- The reopening of Kadim was attended by senior Israeli officials, including Finance Minister Bezalel Smotric and Justice Minister Yariv Levin.
- Two new Israeli military bases are under construction near Jenin, including one on land under full Palestinian Authority control since the 1993 Oslo Accords.

## Implications

- The re-establishment of Kadim and other settlements undermines the viability of a contiguous and sovereign Palestinian state, complicating future peace negotiations.
- Increased settler presence and military infrastructure in the West Bank may lead to heightened friction and potential escalation of violence between Israelis and Palestinians.
- The normalization of settlement expansion could erode international legal norms regarding occupation and territorial acquisition, setting a concerning precedent for other conflicts.

## Outlook

The situation in the West Bank remains volatile, with no immediate signs of de-escalation. As Israel continues to expand settlements and consolidate military presence, Palestinian resistance and international diplomatic pressure are likely to intensify. The coming months will be critical in determining whether the international community can mount a coordinated response or whether the status quo of expansion will persist, further dimming hopes for a negotiated two-state solution.

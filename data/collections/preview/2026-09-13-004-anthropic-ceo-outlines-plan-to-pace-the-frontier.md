# Anthropic CEO Outlines Plan to Pace the Frontier

> [!summary] TL;DR — Anthropic’s Dario Amodei proposes three broad strategies to slow AI development, including third‑party embedded evaluators, coordinated safety standards among democratic AI firms, and limited global coordination with China, while OpenAI and Elon Musk signal support for the “pacing” concept.

## Background

The AI safety debate intensified in September 2026 after researcher Jacob Coxon resigned from Anthropic, warning that leading AI companies are "gambling with our lives." OpenAI CEO Sam Altman has also hinted that the industry should "pace the frontier." In a blog post, Amodei echoed this call, citing the OpenAI‑HuggingFace hack and a rapid acceleration of model capabilities as catalysts for a more cautious approach. His proposals aim to buy time for alignment research while preserving competitive advantage, but they also raise questions about antitrust concerns, geopolitical competition with China, and the practicality of global cooperation.

## Embedded Evaluators as a Trust Mechanism

Amodei’s first concrete step is the unilateral deployment of third‑party evaluators from groups such as METR. These evaluators would be embedded within AI firms, equipped with company badges, desks, and laptops, and granted access comparable to internal risk‑assessment teams. The goal is to verify that companies honor pacing commitments and to ensure safety incidents are reported—highlighted by the recent German wiki forum takeover that OpenAI allegedly failed to disclose. By modeling the arrangement on regulators embedded in banks, Amodei hopes to create an independent audit layer that can both enforce compliance and build public confidence. OpenAI’s Sam Altman has endorsed the idea, indicating that his organization will adopt a similar evaluator framework, though details remain pending.

## Coordinated Safety Standards Among Democratic AI Powers

The second pillar calls for leading AI companies in democratic nations to align on common safety standards and limits on unchecked progress. Amodei acknowledges antitrust worries that a formal pause could trigger regulatory scrutiny, suggesting that the U.S. government should mediate or at least issue a narrow waiver for safety discussions. He also argues that coordinated standards can prevent a race to the bottom while preserving innovation. The proposal implicitly targets China’s rapid AI advancement, positing that a united front could maintain Western leadership. However, the feasibility of such coordination is questionable given existing geopolitical tensions and the competitive nature of the AI market.

## Geopolitical Coordination and Limits on Chinese Advancement

Amodei’s third strategy blends realism with optimism: limited global coordination, including with China, to prohibit narrow but dangerous AI uses such as bioweapon development. He also recommends that the U.S. and its allies restrict the export of powerful chips and semiconductor equipment to Chinese firms, and crack down on model distillation, aiming to slow China’s progress enough to widen America’s lead over the next 3‑5 years. While he admits that “stark limits” exist on what can be achieved with authoritarian regimes, he suggests that even modest agreements on high‑risk applications are worthwhile. This approach reflects a broader shift toward treating AI safety as a matter of national security rather than pure ethics.

## Key facts

- Dario Amodei published a blog post outlining three pacing strategies, committing Anthropic to the first (embedded evaluators).
- Sam Altman of OpenAI confirmed that his company will adopt a similar evaluator model, describing it as a "good idea."
- The proposals follow Jacob Coxon’s resignation from Anthropic, which amplified concerns that AI firms are "gambling with our lives."
- Amodei cites the OpenAI‑HuggingFace hack and a recent surge in model capabilities as reasons for urgent pacing.
- The plan includes coordinated safety standards among democratic AI firms, with a call for U.S. government mediation to address antitrust concerns.
- Geopolitical elements involve restricting chip exports to China and seeking limited cooperation on banning dangerous AI applications.

## Implications

- If embedded evaluators become industry standard, they could set a new baseline for transparency and accountability, potentially reshaping how safety incidents are reported.
- Coordinated standards may face antitrust challenges, but a government‑mediated waiver could create a precedent for regulated collaboration in high‑tech sectors.
- Restricting chip exports and model distillation could slow Chinese AI progress, but may also spur alternative supply chains and accelerate regional AI initiatives.
- The limited outreach to China suggests a pragmatic shift toward risk mitigation over ideological decoupling, though enforcement remains uncertain.

## Outlook

Amodei’s pacing framework is likely to spark both enthusiasm and skepticism. While it offers a concrete roadmap for slowing the AI frontier and addressing safety concerns, its success hinges on widespread adoption, robust evaluator independence, and delicate geopolitical negotiations. The industry’s response—positive from OpenAI and Elon Musk but critical from some AI boosters—indicates a nascent consensus that unchecked acceleration is untenable, yet the path to implementation will be fraught with regulatory, competitive, and international hurdles.

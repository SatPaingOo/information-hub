# German Chancellor Merz calls state election a 'disaster' for his party but vows to stay on

> [!summary] TL;DR — German Chancellor Friedrich Merz branded recent state election losses a "disaster" for the CDU, yet pledged to continue his reform agenda despite mounting pressure and poor poll numbers. The far‑right AfD made strong gains, while Merz’s party fell below the 5 % threshold in Mecklenburg‑Vorpommern and lagged in Berlin, raising questions about his leadership and the coalition’s stability.

## Background

Chancellor Friedrich Merz assumed office in December 2025 after a turbulent period marked by rising populist sentiment and economic uncertainty. His Christian Democratic Union (CDU) had already suffered a major defeat in the Saxony‑Anhalt regional election just two weeks earlier, halving its vote share as the Alternative for Germany (AfD) surged. The recent elections in Mecklenburg‑Vorpommern and Berlin were seen as a litmus test for Merz’s ability to reverse the CDU’s decline and to implement a ambitious package of economic, tax and welfare reforms. The CDU’s poor performance, especially the sub‑5 % result in Mecklenburg‑Vorpommern, triggered internal speculation about a possible mid‑term "chancellor swap" and intensified external pressure from coalition partners and opposition parties alike.

## Political Implications of the Electoral Setback

The CDU’s collapse below the parliamentary threshold in Mecklenburg‑Vorpommern not only deprives the party of a regional voice but also undermines Merz’s narrative of a “course correction” after the Saxony‑Anhalt defeat. This outcome emboldens the AfD, whose co‑leader Alice Weidel declared the party as the “strongest force” and a mainstream representative of voter discontent. The loss also fuels debate within the CDU’s internal power structures: senior figures such as Burkard Dregger argue for continuity, warning that a leadership change would waste precious time before federal reforms can be enacted. Conversely, critics within the party and the broader public question whether Merz’s right‑leaning stance on immigration and his proposed economic reforms can regain lost ground without further alienating centrist and left‑leaning electorates.

## Electoral Dynamics and Regional Particulars

In Mecklenburg‑Vorpommern, the AfD’s projected 38 % share reflects the historic east‑German trend of protest voting against established parties, compounded by economic grievances in a region still grappling with post‑reunification challenges. The CDU’s sub‑5 % result is a stark reversal from its previous standing, highlighting the effectiveness of the AfD’s anti‑immigration, German‑first messaging in a region where cultural identity remains a potent issue. Berlin’s contest, where the CDU slipped to 20 % behind Die Linke’s near‑26 % and the AfD holding around 16 %, underscores a different dynamic: the capital’s more diverse electorate penalizes the CDU for perceived neglect of social housing and climate policies. The fragmentation of the left‑right spectrum suggests a new equilibrium where coalition‑building at the state level will be increasingly complex, with the AfD likely excluded from government due to cross‑party refusal to cooperate.

## Strategic Outlook for Merz’s Government

Merz’s vow to press ahead with reforms signals a high‑risk, high‑reward strategy. He frames the legislative package as an “antidote to authoritarian poison,” attempting to position his government as the bulwark against extremist populism while appealing to moderate voters. However, the timing is precarious: the reforms must navigate a skeptical Bundestag, a public fatigued by successive electoral shocks, and a looming budget crisis. If successful, the agenda could re‑establish the CDU’s credibility and marginalize the AfD’s narrative. If not, the party faces a potential leadership challenge, with internal factions already discussing a possible “chancellor swap” to rejuvenate the party’s image. The next six months will be decisive, as Merz balances the need for decisive action against the political reality of a fragmented parliament and an increasingly impatient electorate.

## Key facts

- Exit polls showed the CDU falling below the 5 % threshold in Mecklenburg‑Vorpommern, marking its worst regional result ever.
- The AfD is projected to win roughly 38 % of the vote in Mecklenburg‑Vorpommern and around 16 % in Berlin.
- Merz announced he will continue with a package of economic, tax and welfare reforms despite the electoral losses.
- The CDU’s vote share in Berlin dropped to about 20 %, placing third behind Die Linke (≈26 %) and the AfD (≈16 %).
- Internal CDU voices, including Burkard Dregger, have publicly supported Merz’s continued leadership.

## Implications

- Weakened CDU standing may force coalition renegotiations at the federal level, potentially altering the balance of power in the Bundestag.
- AfD’s growing popularity could pressure mainstream parties to adopt tougher immigration and cultural preservation policies to curb further erosion.
- Merz’s reform agenda faces heightened scrutiny; failure could trigger a leadership challenge and destabilize the government.
- Regional political fragmentation may lead to more frequent minority governments or caretaker administrations, affecting policy continuity.

## Outlook

The electoral setbacks constitute a pivotal moment for Chancellor Merz’s tenure. While the CDU’s decline underscores deep-seated voter discontent, Merz’s determination to press forward with reforms offers a potential pathway to re‑establish political relevance. The coming months will test whether his strategic vision can reconcile the demands of a disillusioned electorate with the pragmatic realities of coalition governance. Should the reforms deliver tangible economic improvements, Merz may consolidate a new centrist coalition and marginalize the AfD. Conversely, continued poor performance could precipitate a leadership crisis, reshaping the German political landscape and influencing broader European trends in populist versus mainstream politics.

# Trump says AI safety fears a 'hoax' as he rejects calls for greater safeguards

> [!summary] TL;DR — President Trump dismissed AI safety concerns as a hoax, likening them to the 'Global Warming Scam' and 'Russia hoax', while tech leaders and lawmakers debate mandatory kill switches and regulation.

## Background

The AI safety debate has intensified in recent weeks, sparked by warnings from senior figures at leading AI firms about existential risks. Anthropic co‑founder Jack Clark called for a third‑party‑verifiable kill switch, and Microsoft released a ‘humanist AI’ framework. These industry moves have collided with sharply polarized political rhetoric, most notably from U.S. President Donald Trump, who framed safety concerns as a hoax and urged a hands‑off approach. The clash highlights a growing divide between rapid technological advancement, calls for governance, and the geopolitical competition with China, where policymakers worry that over‑regulation could cede strategic advantage.

## Political Rhetoric vs. Technical Reality

Trump’s characterization of AI safety fears as a hoax follows a pattern of dismissing scientific consensus that conflicts with his policy agenda. By equating AI safeguards with the “Global Warming Scam” and the “Russia hoax,” he seeks to delegitimize regulatory pressure and rally his base around unfettered innovation. This rhetoric can sway public opinion, complicate bipartisan legislative efforts, and embolden industry players who prefer self‑regulation, potentially delaying meaningful safeguards that many technical experts consider urgent.

## Industry Moves Toward Safeguards

Despite Trump’s stance, the private sector is increasingly internalizing safety concerns. Anthropic’s Jack Clark advocated a mandatory kill‑switch that can be audited by an independent party, noting that most labs already have ad‑hoc shutdown capabilities. Microsoft’s release of a “humanist AI” outline and CEO Mustafa Suleyman’s confirmation that the guidance was driven by the current debate illustrate a shift toward ethical design principles. Other major players—OpenAI’s Sam Altman, xAI’s Elon Musk, and Anthropic’s Dario Amodei—have called for a slower development pace and independent monitoring, though they stress preserving commercial advantage. These moves suggest a nascent industry consensus that self‑regulation may be preferable to government mandates, yet the lack of uniform standards leaves gaps.

## Geopolitical Implications and Competitive Dynamics

The U.S. debate occurs against a backdrop of intensifying AI rivalry with China. Chinese state media have warned that Washington’s growing anxiety over China’s rapid AI progress is distorting policy priorities, potentially giving Beijing a strategic edge if the U.S. slows development or imposes strict regulation. Analysts such as Xin Qiang of Fudan University argue that the U.S. must balance safety with competitiveness. Meanwhile, the global tech community watches for regulatory signals that could affect investment flows, talent migration, and the emergence of international standards. The upcoming “Pro‑Human Assembly,” featuring figures as divergent as Steve Bannon and Senator Bernie Sanders, underscores the cross‑party concern that uncontrolled AI growth could pose an existential threat, further complicating diplomatic coordination.

## Key facts

- Trump labeled AI safety fears a hoax and compared them to the Global Warming Scam and Russia hoax.
- Anthropic co‑founder Jack Clark suggested a mandatory third‑party‑verifiable kill switch for dangerous AI.
- Microsoft released a ‘humanist AI’ framework and CEO Mustafa Suleyman said the guidance was prompted by the current debate.
- Former Trump strategist Steve Bannon and Senator Bernie Sanders will speak at the Pro‑Human Assembly, calling for a re‑evaluation of AI policy.
- Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei called for a slower pace of AI development and tighter monitoring while preserving commercial advantage.

## Implications

- U.S. policy may stall or reverse, emboldening a laissez‑faire approach that could hinder international safety standards.
- Investor reactions—tech share sell‑offs—signal market uncertainty about regulatory slowdowns and potential competitive gaps.

## Outlook

The AI safety discourse is likely to remain polarized, with Trump’s rhetoric continuing to push for deregulation while industry leaders push for self‑imposed guardrails. Incremental regulation may emerge as a compromise, driven by bipartisan concern over existential risk and the strategic imperative to stay competitive with China. The ultimate framework will probably blend voluntary industry standards with limited government oversight, shaping a global benchmark for AI governance.

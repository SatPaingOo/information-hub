# Houthis say they targeted Saudi capital with ballistic missiles

> [!summary] TL;DR — Iran‑backed Houthi militants launched drone and ballistic missile strikes on Riyadh and Red Sea energy sites, prompting Saudi air‑defence intercepts and temporary airport disruptions. The US warned Americans of heightened travel risks, while the conflict threatens regional oil markets and could escalate further.

## Background

The Houthi‑Saudi confrontation has intensified since July 2024, with the Houthis seizing strategic ports such as Mocha and Perim Island, tightening a naval blockade that challenges Saudi maritime exports. Saudi Arabia’s reliance on Red Sea shipping routes has grown after the de‑facto closure of the Strait of Hormuz amid the US‑Israel war with Iran. This backdrop makes any attack on Saudi infrastructure, especially fuel depots and airports, a potential catalyst for broader market and security shocks.

## Escalation of Houthi‑Saudi Hostilities

The Houthi military spokesman, Yahya al‑Sarea, announced the use of a "large number of ballistic and cruise missiles and drones" against "sensitive sites" in Riyadh and Aramco facilities in Yanbu. This marks the first air‑raid alerts for Riyadh since the Houthis stepped up attacks in July, signalling a tactical shift from peripheral Saudi targets to the capital itself. The strikes, though limited, were enough to trigger black‑smoke plumes over a fuel depot at King Khalid International Airport, causing temporary flight cancellations and grounding. Saudi authorities confirmed the interception of at least one ballistic missile over Riyadh, while air‑defences also reported successes over Bish, Taif, Farasan and Yanbu, underscoring the kingdom’s layered missile‑defence posture.

## Strategic Energy Infrastructure at Risk

The targeted fuel depot is a critical node in Saudi Arabia’s domestic fuel supply chain, feeding both the capital’s aviation sector and regional distribution networks. Disruption here can ripple through airline schedules, increase fuel prices domestically, and affect the kingdom’s ability to export refined products via Red Sea ports. The Houthis’ focus on Aramco’s coastal facilities in Yanbu highlights the vulnerability of Saudi energy assets that have become an indirect front in the broader proxy conflict with Iran. Analysts note that the attack coincides with heightened uncertainty in global oil markets, as JP Morgan’s rare investor note warned of the difficulty in modelling the conflict’s endgame, suggesting that any sustained disruption could accelerate price volatility.

## International Response and Deterrence Calculus

The U.S. State Department issued an advisory urging Americans across the Middle East to "exercise vigilance" and anticipate travel disruptions, while explicitly linking the attacks to Iranian support for the Houthis. This warning reflects Washington’s concern that the conflict could rapidly expand, especially given the involvement of civilian airports and critical energy infrastructure. The Saudi‑led coalition’s air‑defence successes were lauded as a deterrent, yet the Houthis’ ability to penetrate Saudi airspace demonstrates evolving capabilities, likely bolstered by Iranian technology transfers. Diplomatic channels are reportedly active, but the immediate outlook leans toward continued tit‑for‑tat strikes unless a credible de‑escalation framework is negotiated.

## Key facts

- Houthi forces claimed responsibility for drone and ballistic missile attacks on Riyadh and Aramco sites in Yanbu.
- Saudi air‑defences intercepted at least one ballistic missile over Riyadh and reported successes over four other cities.
- A fuel depot at King Khalid International Airport erupted in flames, causing temporary flight disruptions and black‑smoke visible from the capital.
- The U.S. State Department warned Americans of heightened travel risks and urged reconsideration of non‑essential travel to the region.
- Houthis recently seized the strategic port city of Mocha and Perim Island, tightening a naval blockade on Red Sea shipping lanes used by Saudi oil exports.
- JP Morgan’s recent analyst note highlighted the difficulty of modelling oil‑price impacts from the ongoing war, signalling market uncertainty.

## Implications

- Potential spikes in global oil prices if Red Sea export routes face sustained disruption.
- Increased risk to civilian populations and critical infrastructure in Saudi Arabia, prompting tighter security measures and possible retaliatory cycles.
- U.S. policy may shift toward more robust defensive assistance to Saudi Arabia, while diplomatic efforts intensify to prevent a broader regional war.

## Outlook

The immediate threat level remains elevated, with both sides demonstrating willingness to target high‑value assets. While Saudi air‑defence capabilities have mitigated direct harm to the capital, the psychological impact and economic fallout are already evident. Over the coming weeks, markets will watch for any further strikes, the response of international actors, and whether diplomatic channels can produce a cease‑fire or confidence‑building measures. A failure to de‑escalate could see the conflict expand to include additional oil infrastructure, further tightening global energy supplies.

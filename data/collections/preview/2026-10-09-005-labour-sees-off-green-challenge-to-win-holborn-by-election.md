# Labour sees off Green challenge to win Holborn by-election

> [!summary] TL;DR — Labour’s Sagal Abdi‑Wali secured a narrow victory in the Holborn and St Pancras by‑election, beating Green Party leader Zack Polanski and reducing the party’s majority. The result underscores Labour’s continued dominance in inner‑London while exposing internal tensions within the Greens over Zionism and leadership.

## Background

The by‑election was triggered by the resignation of former Prime Minister Sir Keir Starmer as MP for Holborn and St Pancras. It was framed as a key test of Labour’s ability, under Andy Burnham’s stewardship, to retain voters who have drifted toward the Greens, especially after strong local gains by the Greens in May 2026. The Green Party, riding a post‑leadership surge following Zack Polanski’s appointment in September 2025, hoped to overturn a historic Labour majority that has never fallen below 40 % in any general election since the seat’s creation in 1983. Simultaneously, the Greens were embroiled in controversy over a motion passed at their annual conference declaring Zionism a form of racism, a stance that sparked criticism from former leader Caroline Lucas and raised questions about Polanski’s capacity to navigate the party’s ideological divides.

## Electoral Dynamics and Voter Shifts

Although Labour retained the seat, its majority fell dramatically from 11,572 to 3,364 votes, reflecting a 22 % increase in Green share compared with the 2024 general election. This erosion signals growing disillusionment among inner‑London constituents with Labour’s performance, despite the party’s continued brand strength. The surge in Green support appears linked to concerns over housing affordability, rent controls, and climate policy—issues that resonated strongly in a constituency with a high concentration of young professionals and renters. The result also suggests that the Greens have successfully tapped into anti‑incumbent sentiment, even if they fell short of winning the seat.

## Green Party Internal Struggles

Polanski’s decision to remain as leader despite the defeat highlights a party at a crossroads. The controversy surrounding the Zionism motion has deepened fractures, with senior figures such as Caroline Lucas accusing him of lacking decisive leadership. The motion, which frames Zionism as an ethnonationalist ideology and calls for a single democratic Palestinian state, has alienated both centrist voters and Jewish community leaders, potentially limiting the Greens’ broader electoral appeal. Moreover, the party’s recent rise in popularity appears to have plateaued, as recent polls indicate waning enthusiasm, suggesting that internal discord may be offsetting policy-driven momentum.

## Implications for UK Policy Landscape

Labour’s narrow win reinforces the party’s status as the default governing force in London but forces a recalibration of its policy priorities. The reduced majority signals that voters are demanding more concrete action on housing, rent controls, and climate resilience—issues championed by the Greens. For the Conservatives, the result is a warning that complacency in traditionally safe seats could backfire, especially as Reform UK and the Liberal Democrats continue to nibble at the edges. On the broader stage, the episode underscores the growing influence of progressive parties in shaping mainstream policy debates, a trend that aligns with wider global shifts toward more aggressive climate and housing agendas.

## Key facts

- Labour’s Sagal Abdi‑Wali won 45 % of the vote; Green leader Zack Polanski secured 33 %.
- The Conservative candidate, Ewan Cameron, placed third with 7 % (1,922 votes).
- The Green Party’s vote share rose 22 % relative to the 2024 general election.
- Labour’s majority fell from 11,572 to 3,364 votes, the smallest margin in the seat’s history.
- Polanski faced criticism over his handling of a motion branding Zionism a form of racism, prompting calls for leadership review.

## Implications

- Labour must address housing and rent‑control concerns to prevent further erosion of its voter base.
- The Greens’ internal divisions could limit their ability to capitalize on discontent with mainstream parties.
- The by‑election outcome may influence the positioning of the Conservatives, Reform UK, and Liberal Democrats in upcoming local and national contests.

## Outlook

Looking ahead, the Holborn result is likely to prompt Labour to fine‑tune its policy platform, emphasizing tangible improvements in affordability and climate action to reassure swing voters. The Green Party will need to reconcile its ideological stance on Israel/Palestine with electoral pragmatism, potentially moderating the rhetoric to broaden appeal. Meanwhile, the broader UK political environment remains fluid, with housing affordability and climate policy set to dominate the next election cycle, ensuring that the dynamics observed in Holborn continue to shape party strategies nationwide.

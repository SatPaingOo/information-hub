# North Korean hackers used fake video calls in $400m crypto hack, says FBI

> [!summary] TL;DR — The FBI has identified North Korean hackers operating the FaceStealer scheme, which used fake video calls to steal identities and $400 million from cryptocurrency victims over 18 months. The scheme targets cryptocurrency and web3 professionals, and is linked to North Korea's funding of illicit weapons programs.

## Background

The FBI's cyber director Brett Leatherman stated that North Korean regime proceeds from large-scale cryptocurrency theft fund its illicit weapons program. This follows a week after Binance revealed a record $6.3 billion hack attributed to North Korea-linked actors. The FaceStealer scheme is described as a highly effective social engineering operation that exploits trust in video conference platforms to deceive victims into downloading malware.

## The FaceStealer Attack Mechanism

The FaceStealer scheme operates through a multi-stage social engineering attack. Hackers hijack or compromise social media accounts to invite victims into impersonated video conference calls (fake Zoom, Teams, or Meet sessions). The attackers play pre-recorded videos of previous victims to simulate legitimacy, but the audio quality is deliberately degraded. A prompt appears asking the victim to 'fix' the audio by clicking a link, which downloads malware onto the device. This malware grants access to contact lists, apps, and other sensitive data, enabling further scams against the victim's network. The FBI describes this as a 'highly effective social engineering scheme capable of deceiving even the most experienced technical and security conscious computer user.'

## Geopolitical Implications for North Korea

The FBI's attribution of the FaceStealer scheme to North Korean hackers reveals a direct link between state-sponsored cyber operations and the regime's broader strategic objectives. The agency states that proceeds from such large-scale cryptocurrency theft fund North Korea's illicit weapons program. This represents a significant escalation in the regime's use of cybercrime to support military capabilities, particularly given the ongoing conflict in Ukraine and other regional tensions. The scheme's focus on cryptocurrency and web3 professionals suggests a deliberate targeting of the digital economy sector to maximize financial gain while avoiding traditional banking detection.

## Impact on the Crypto and Open-Source Industries

The FaceStealer scheme has affected over 900 victims in the past 18 months, primarily targeting those in the cryptocurrency and web3 sectors. The FBI notes that the hackers specifically target developers and maintainers of open-source software, who are critical to the ecosystem's security and stability. By compromising these professionals, the attackers gain access to secure development environments, private keys, and internal communications. This represents a threat to the integrity of the global crypto infrastructure and could facilitate further cyberattacks on financial institutions and other critical sectors. The scheme also demonstrates how state-sponsored actors exploit the growing digital economy to fund military operations.

## Key facts

- The FBI identifies North Korean hackers as the perpetrators of the FaceStealer scheme
- Over 900 victims have been affected by the FaceStealer scheme in the last 18 months
- The scheme generates approximately $400 million in cryptocurrency theft
- FaceStealer targets cryptocurrency and web3 professionals, particularly open-source software developers
- The FBI attributes the scheme to North Korea, linking it to the regime's funding of illicit weapons programs
- A previous major hack at Binance (record $6.3 billion) was also attributed to North Korea-linked actors

## Implications

- The FaceStealer scheme represents a sophisticated evolution of state-sponsored cybercrime targeting the digital economy
- North Korea's use of cryptocurrency theft to fund weapons programs raises serious international security concerns
- The attack on open-source software developers threatens the security and resilience of the global crypto ecosystem
- The scale of the operation (900+ victims, $400 million) indicates significant resources and organizational capacity
- The FBI's attribution provides important intelligence for international countermeasures against North Korean cyber activities

## Outlook

The FBI continues to investigate the FaceStealer operation and its connections to North Korea's broader cyber warfare strategy. The attackers may adapt their tactics to evade detection, and the use of open-source software as a target suggests ongoing vulnerabilities in the global digital infrastructure. International cooperation between US and Korean authorities will be critical in disrupting this operation and preventing further financial losses. The case also highlights the growing intersection between cybercrime, state sponsorship, and military funding in modern geopolitical conflicts.

# Badenoch says she is instructing lawyers to challenge government after Tory-run council's 94% tax hike

> [!summary] TL;DR — Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch has commissioned lawyers to contest a £84 million annual funding cut that triggered a near‑doubling of council tax in Wandsworth, warning families will feel the pain despite the borough’s historically low rates.

## Background

Wandsworth, a south‑west London borough governed by the Tories, has long boasted one of the lowest council tax bills in the capital—around £1,000 for a Band D property. A funding formula change announced last year re‑allocates resources from areas that traditionally vote Conservative toward Labour‑strongholds, stripping Wandsworth of roughly £84 million per year by the end of the next three years. The council’s proposed £958 increase—almost a 94 % rise—sparked fierce criticism from residents, opposition councillors, and even Labour MPs who argue the borough was financially healthy before the Tories assumed power in May 2024. Badenoch’s announcement that the Conservative Party is “instructing lawyers” to examine the legality of the funding cut signals a potential judicial showdown over fiscal equity and central‑government authority.

## Political Context and Funding Formula

The funding shift is framed by the government as a correction of an “outdated funding system” to direct money where need is greatest, yet critics see it as politically motivated redistribution. By targeting a council that has historically resisted higher taxes and maintained low rates, the policy appears to punish a Tory stronghold while rewarding Labour‑leaning areas. This raises questions about the impartiality of the new formula and whether fiscal adjustments are being used as tools for electoral advantage, especially with the upcoming local elections looming.

## Legal and Fiscal Implications for Wandsworth

Badenoch’s legal review could hinge on arguments that the funding cut breaches statutory guidance on local authority financing or constitutes an unlawful interference with council autonomy. If successful, a court could order the restoration of funds, forcing the government to either reverse the cut or compensate Wandsworth. Conversely, a defeat would cement the central government’s power to reshape local budgets, setting a precedent that may affect other councils facing similar reductions, especially those in Tory‑led areas.

## Broader Impact on Local Governance and Party Dynamics

The dispute highlights a growing tension between central fiscal policy and local self‑determination. Other London boroughs—Westminster, Richmond, Kensington & Chelsea, and Windsor & Maidenhead—have already signalled intent to invoke the Sustainable Communities Act to contest the cuts, suggesting a coordinated front against the funding rebalancing. Within the Conservative Party, Badenoch’s stance may be an attempt to reclaim the narrative of fiscal responsibility and protect traditional Tory voter bases, while Labour MPs in Wandsworth have distanced themselves from the council’s appeal, underscoring intra‑party fissures over local governance.

## Key facts

- Wandsworth Council proposes a £958 council tax rise—nearly a 94 % increase—bringing average Band D bills to over £1,900.
- The borough stands to lose £84 million annually in central government funding by the end of the next three years due to last year’s funding formula change.
- Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch has confirmed the Conservative Party is “instructing lawyers” to explore challenging the legality of the funding cut.
- The government defends the changes as a realignment of funding toward areas of greatest need, lifting the usual 5 % cap on council tax rises for affected authorities.
- Three Labour MPs in Wandsworth—Rosena Allin‑Khan, Fleur Anderson, and Marsha de Cordova—have refused to back the council’s appeal, citing the borough’s pre‑Tory financial health.

## Implications

- A judicial win for the Conservatives could limit future central government power to reallocate local funds, reshaping fiscal policy nationwide.
- If the legal challenge fails, other councils may face similar tax hikes, intensifying public discontent and potentially influencing upcoming local elections.
- The funding rebalancing may deepen political polarisation, with Tory‑led areas feeling penalised and Labour‑stronghold areas benefiting, affecting voter sentiment.
- The dispute could prompt broader reforms of the council funding formula, possibly leading to a more transparent and politically neutral allocation mechanism.

## Outlook

The legal battle will likely unfold over the coming months, with court hearings expected to address both the procedural legitimacy of the funding cut and its substantive impact on Wandsworth residents. Regardless of the outcome, the case will set an important precedent for the balance of power between central and local government in the UK. In the interim, Wandsworth households face the prospect of near‑doubling council tax bills, pressuring family budgets and potentially driving a wave of political mobilisation ahead of next year’s local elections. Observers will be watching to see whether the government’s “fixing an outdated system” narrative holds up under judicial scrutiny or if the courts will compel a more equitable approach to local financing.

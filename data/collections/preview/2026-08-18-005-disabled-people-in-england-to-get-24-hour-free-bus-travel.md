# Disabled people in England to get 24-hour free bus travel

> [!summary] TL;DR — From April, disabled people in England will be able to use their free bus passes at any time of day, removing previous weekday time restrictions. The £60m scheme, funded by the Department for Transport and Department for Work and Pensions, is expected to benefit around one million people and aligns with broader cost-of-living and employment initiatives.

## Background

Disabled bus passes in England have historically been restricted to use between 09:30 and 23:00 on weekdays, limiting access for those needing to travel to work or attend evening events. Weekend and bank holiday travel was unrestricted. The policy change follows advocacy by disability groups and mirrors reforms previously implemented by Prime Minister Andy Burnham during his tenure as Mayor of Greater Manchester. Similar unrestricted free travel schemes already exist in Scotland, Wales, and parts of Northern Ireland.

## Policy Motivation and Political Context

The decision to extend free bus travel to 24 hours for disabled people reflects a convergence of social equity and economic policy objectives. Prime Minister Andy Burnham frames the reform as a moral imperative, arguing that time-restricted passes create unnecessary barriers to employment and social participation. This aligns with the government's broader cost-of-living agenda, which has included measures such as free bus travel for under-16s during August and a £2 single-fare cap. The policy also serves a political function, reinforcing the Labour government's commitment to inclusive public services and potentially bolstering support among disabled voters ahead of future electoral cycles.

## Financial Implications and Fiscal Responsibility

The £60m annual cost of the scheme is to be absorbed by reallocating budgets within the Department for Transport and the Department for Work and Pensions. While the government describes the commitment as indefinite, Work and Pensions Minister Lilian Greenwood acknowledged that long-term funding sustainability will be evaluated during upcoming spending reviews. Critics from the Conservative Party have questioned the fiscal prudence of the move, suggesting it represents budgetary reshuffling rather than new investment. The financial model relies on the assumption that increased bus usage will not disproportionately strain operator revenues, particularly given existing subsidies and fare caps.

## Social Impact and Equity Considerations

Disability advocacy organizations, including Scope and Transport for All, have welcomed the reform as a significant step toward reducing systemic inequities in public transportation. By eliminating time-based restrictions, the policy enhances mobility for approximately one million disabled individuals, facilitating access to employment, healthcare, and leisure activities. The removal of a 'postcode lottery'—wherein different regions applied varying rules—standardizes access across England and underscores the government's intent to harmonize public services. However, the effectiveness of the policy will ultimately depend on the reliability and accessibility of bus services in rural and underserved areas, where transport infrastructure remains inadequate.

## Key facts

- The policy will take effect on 1 April, extending free bus travel to 24 hours for disabled people in England.
- Approximately one million people are expected to benefit from the removal of weekday time restrictions on disabled bus passes.
- The £60m annual cost will be covered by budgets from the Department for Transport and the Department for Work and Pensions.
- Similar unrestricted free travel schemes already operate in Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland for certain groups.
- The reform follows similar changes introduced by Prime Minister Andy Burnham in Greater Manchester.

## Implications

- Increased accessibility to employment and social activities for disabled individuals, potentially improving economic participation and quality of life.
- Greater standardization of transport policy across the UK, reducing regional disparities in access to public services.
- Potential strain on bus operators if usage increases significantly, particularly during off-peak hours.
- Heightened political scrutiny of government spending, with opposition parties challenging the fiscal sustainability of the policy.

## Outlook

The success of the 24-hour free bus travel policy will hinge on its implementation and the responsiveness of local transport authorities to increased demand. While the reform is positioned as an indefinite commitment, future spending reviews may test the government's resolve to maintain funding levels. Long-term sustainability will require careful monitoring of ridership patterns and continued investment in bus infrastructure, particularly in areas where service frequency and accessibility remain suboptimal. The policy may also set a precedent for further expansions of free travel initiatives, potentially influencing devolved administrations and shaping the national discourse on transport equity.

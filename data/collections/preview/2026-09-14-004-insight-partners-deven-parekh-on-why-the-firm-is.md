# Insight Partners’ Deven Parekh on why the firm is diversifying while everyone else bets the farm on OpenAI and Anthropic

> [!summary] TL;DR — Deven Parekh explains Insight Partners’ deliberate diversification in AI, despite the sector’s concentration on OpenAI and Anthropic, citing balanced risk, stage‑dependent investing, and a focus on portfolio performance over headline‑grabbing bets.

## Background

Insight Partners, a $90 billion‑plus investment vehicle co‑run by Deven Parekh for 26 years, has maintained a notably low‑profile stance compared with peers. While many venture firms are pouring capital into frontier AI labs, Insight continues to spread its exposure across early‑stage, growth, and buyout opportunities worldwide. In a candid sit‑down at StrictlyVC, Parekh discussed the firm’s dual stakes in rival AI giants OpenAI and Anthropic, the loss of the AI legal‑tech startup Legora to General Catalyst, and why Insight’s portfolio‑first philosophy outweighs the allure of concentrating on the latest AI hype. The interview underscores a broader debate in venture capital about risk management, stage‑specific constraints, and the tension between following market trends and preserving a diversified investment narrative.

## Diversification Rationale and Portfolio Discipline

Parekh frames Insight’s approach as a reaction to the herd mentality that often characterizes venture capital, especially in high‑profile AI cycles. He emphasizes that the firm’s $90 billion asset base is not a mandate to chase the next big AI lab but to allocate capital where founders and companies demonstrate sustainable traction. By keeping a broad geographic and vertical spread—early‑stage, growth, and occasional buyouts—Insight avoids the volatility that can arise from over‑concentration in a single model or infrastructure play. This disciplined stance is reinforced by a belief that performance should speak for itself rather than relying on social‑media buzz or podcast appearances.

## Dual Stakes in OpenAI and Anthropic: Strategic Necessity or Conflict?

Insight’s simultaneous investments in OpenAI and Anthropic, once considered a taboo, are rationalized by Parekh as stage‑dependent decisions. Early‑stage exclusivity rules prevent the firm from backing directly competing companies, yet at later stages—where governance is off the board—these positions become akin to buying proven stocks. The firm viewed OpenAI as the dominant consumer‑facing play and Anthropic as the clear enterprise‑focused contender, a dynamic that has shifted in real time as both labs raised tens of billions. The dual exposure illustrates Insight’s willingness to accept perceived conflicts when the risk is mitigated by the investment’s maturity and the firm’s passive ownership role.

## Market Dynamics and Competitive Landscape

Parekh notes that AI infrastructure talent is heavily concentrated in San Francisco, prompting his son—a VC himself—to relocate there for access to deals. However, vertical AI opportunities (e.g., financial‑services AI like Ramp) remain geographically dispersed, allowing Insight to maintain a global footprint. The loss of Legora to General Catalyst underscores the competitive intensity of AI deals, where firm reputation and value‑ proposition articulation can tip the scales. Moreover, the rapid pace of fundraising in frontier AI has eroded exclusivity clauses, forcing investors to adapt to a market where multiple large‑scale labs can coexist without clear dominance.

## Risk Management and Governance Considerations

Beyond financial risk, Parekh highlights AI‑related existential concerns, such as the potential misuse of open‑source models for bioweapons. He balances these worries against the transformative potential of AI in healthcare—citing NYU Langone’s use of patient data to predict heart‑attack risk—and argues that societal benefits outweigh the hazards. Insight’s governance approach focuses on stage‑appropriate information sharing, avoiding direct competition, and maintaining a passive stance once a portfolio company reaches a certain size. This framework aims to protect both the firm and its founders from undue influence while still capturing upside in high‑growth AI ventures.

## Key facts

- Insight Partners manages roughly $90 billion in assets and has a 26‑year track record co‑led by Deven Parekh.
- The firm holds stakes in both OpenAI and Anthropic, a rare dual‑investment in rival AI labs.
- Insight lost the AI legal‑tech startup Legora to General Catalyst after a competitive bidding process.
- Parekh emphasizes a portfolio‑first, diversified strategy rather than concentrating on frontier AI labs.
- The firm’s investment split varies temporally; buyouts have been scarce since 2024 due to high rates and tighter credit.
- Talent concentration for AI infrastructure is centered in San Francisco, while vertical AI talent remains globally dispersed.
- OpenAI and Anthropic raised roughly half of all VC‑backed AI capital in recent rounds, reshaping exclusivity norms.

## Implications

- Other large VCs may reconsider aggressive concentration in a single AI lab, favoring a balanced approach that spreads risk across stages and verticals.
- The dual‑stake model could become more common if later‑stage AI investments are viewed primarily as passive equity rather than active governance.
- Geographically diversified AI investing may gain traction as talent clusters remain uneven across different AI verticals.
- Regulatory scrutiny of AI risk may increase pressure on investors to demonstrate responsible exposure, potentially influencing future deal structures.

## Outlook

Insight Partners’ stance suggests a maturing AI investment ecosystem where size and scale no longer guarantee dominance. By maintaining a diversified portfolio and adhering to stage‑specific governance, Insight positions itself to capture upside across multiple AI paradigms while mitigating systemic risk. This approach may influence peers to adopt similar disciplined frameworks, especially as the AI market transitions from rapid, hype‑driven fundraising to more sustainable, long‑term value creation.

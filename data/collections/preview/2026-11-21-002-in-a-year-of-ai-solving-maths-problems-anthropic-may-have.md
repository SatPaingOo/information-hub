# In a year of AI solving maths problems, Anthropic may have just solved the biggest

> [!summary] TL;DR — Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge claims to have used a non‑public version of the Claude chatbot to produce a 250,000‑line Lean proof of the Riemann hypothesis, the most famous unsolved problem in mathematics. Verification by the community, including Fields medallist Terence Tao, will take months. The announcement coincides with Anthropic’s stock‑market debut, raising questions about timing, credibility, and the broader impact of AI‑driven mathematical discovery.

## Background

The Riemann hypothesis, posed in 1859, asserts a precise distribution of the non‑trivial zeros of the zeta function and underpins much of modern number theory and cryptography. For decades it has resisted proof despite intense effort. In 2024‑2025, large language models (LLMs) from OpenAI, Google DeepMind, and Anthropic began claiming breakthroughs on long‑standing problems such as the Navier‑Stokes equations and dozens of Olympiad‑level conjectures. These claims have sparked debate over verification standards, the role of human oversight, and whether commercial AI labs are using high‑profile mathematics as a marketing lever. Anthropic’s Claude Opus 4.7, a proprietary LLM, was the tool Alpöge employed over six weeks, producing a formal proof in Lean, a proof‑assistant language that enables machine‑checked verification.

## Credibility and Verification Dynamics

The proof’s length — over 250,000 lines of Lean code — makes manual audit impractical; verification will rely on a combination of automated proof‑checking and human expert review. Terence Tao’s cautious endorsement highlights a new collaborative model: a human author (Alpöge) can guide reviewers through the AI‑generated structure, unlike earlier OpenAI releases where no human intermediary was identified. However, the timing — announced the day after Anthropic’s IPO — fuels skepticism about whether the claim is a genuine scientific milestone or a strategic publicity stunt. The community’s response will set a precedent for how future AI‑generated proofs are evaluated, especially regarding transparency of the model version, training data, and prompt engineering used.

## Commercial Incentives vs. Scientific Norms

Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google are racing to demonstrate that their LLMs can tackle "grand challenges" in mathematics, physics, and computer science. Publicly announcing solutions before peer review serves marketing and investor relations, as evidenced by the stock‑listing coincidence. A group of senior mathematicians warned that such "boasting" threatens the integrity of mathematics by conflating computational output with rigorous proof. The tension mirrors earlier debates over AI‑generated art and literature, but the stakes are higher because mathematical proofs underpin cryptographic security, algorithmic complexity theory, and scientific modelling. Policy makers may need to consider disclosure requirements for AI‑assisted discoveries, akin to clinical‑trial registration.

## Implications for Agentic AI and Formal Verification

Alpöge’s workflow — iteratively prompting a non‑public Claude model, refining Lean statements, and assembling a massive proof script — exemplifies an emerging "agentic AI" paradigm where LLMs act as semi‑autonomous research assistants. The success of this approach could accelerate formal verification in software engineering, hardware design, and safety‑critical systems, where Lean and Coq are already used. Yet the reliance on proprietary, opaque models raises reproducibility concerns: other researchers cannot replicate the exact reasoning path without access to the same model version. Open‑source alternatives (e.g., Hugging Face’s LLaMA‑based math‑tuned models) may democratise the capability, but they currently lag behind the performance of closed‑source Opus‑class systems.

## Geopolitical and Regulatory Outlook

If AI‑driven proofs become routine, nations may treat advanced mathematical capability as a strategic asset, similar to quantum computing. Export controls on frontier models, data‑center localisation, and talent retention could become policy levers. The EU’s AI Act and the U.S. Executive Order on AI safety already hint at classification of "high‑impact" AI systems; a verified Riemann proof would likely trigger a reassessment of what constitutes a "dual‑use" capability. International bodies such as the International Mathematical Union may need to establish certification standards for AI‑assisted proofs to preserve trust across borders.

## Key facts

- Levent Alpöge, an Anthropic mathematician, claims a full Lean proof of the Riemann hypothesis generated with a non‑public Claude Opus 4.7 model over six weeks.
- The proof comprises >250,000 lines of Lean code; verification by experts including Terence Tao is expected to take months.
- Announcement coincided with Anthropic’s U.S. stock‑market debut, prompting timing criticism.
- OpenAI previously claimed AI‑assisted progress on Navier‑Stokes and 372 other problems, none yet peer‑reviewed.
- A collective of mathematicians warned that premature commercial claims jeopardise mathematical rigor.

## Implications

- Verification pipelines will need hybrid human‑AI review frameworks, potentially standardised by bodies like the IMU.
- Commercial AI labs may face pressure to disclose model versions, training data, and prompt logs for high‑profile claims.
- Agentic AI workflows could reshape formal methods in software/hardware verification, but proprietary access limits reproducibility.
- Governments may classify advanced math‑capable LLMs as strategic technologies, influencing export‑control regimes.

## Outlook

The next 6‑12 months will be decisive: if the Lean proof withstands community scrutiny, it will mark the first AI‑verified solution to a Millennium Prize problem, catalysing a wave of AI‑assisted formal mathematics and prompting regulatory frameworks for high‑impact AI discoveries. Failure or prolonged ambiguity will reinforce skepticism and may slow commercial incentives for grand‑challenge claims.

# Manchester to host world leaders at G20 summit in 2027

> [!summary] TL;DR — Prime Minister Andy Burnham will welcome US President Donald Trump to Manchester for the 2027 G20 summit, marking the city’s first hosting of the world’s major economic gathering. The event, set for 27‑28 November 2027 at Manchester Central Convention Centre, follows a year‑long UK presidency and signals a shift in global diplomatic visibility.

## Background

The United Kingdom last hosted a G20 summit in London in 2009, incurring an estimated £7.2 million security bill. This year the UK assumed the G20 presidency, with the current summit taking place in Miami in December 2026. Manchester’s selection earlier in 2026—before Andy Burnham became prime minister—reflects a strategic push to decentralise high‑profile international events across the UK. Burnham, a former mayor of Greater Manchester, previously stated he would not welcome a US presidential visit, but his stance has softened as the city positions itself as a global diplomatic hub.

## Logistical Preparations and Economic Impact

Hosting the G20 in Manchester Central Convention Centre will require extensive infrastructure upgrades, including transport links, accommodation, and temporary facilities for delegations and media. The city’s existing convention capabilities, combined with recent investments in digital connectivity, are expected to support high‑speed data transmission and secure communications for world leaders. Economically, the summit is projected to generate billions in indirect spending through hospitality, tourism, and service sectors, while also offering Manchester a platform to showcase its post‑industrial regeneration to a global audience.

## Diplomatic Dynamics and US‑UK Relations

The presence of Donald Trump at a UK‑hosted summit re‑opens delicate diplomatic channels after a period of strained relations under previous administrations. Burnham’s evolution from rejecting a presidential visit to actively welcoming Trump illustrates a pragmatic shift in British foreign policy, prioritising engagement over ideology. The summit provides an opportunity for the UK to reinforce its role as a bridge between the United States, the European Union, and emerging economies, particularly as the G20 agenda will likely address climate finance, trade resilience, and AI governance—issues where both nations have divergent priorities.

## Security and Governance Challenges

Security planning for the 2027 event will dwarf previous UK counter‑terrorism operations, drawing on lessons from London 2009 and recent domestic protests. The estimated cost of the security operation is expected to exceed £10 million, reflecting heightened threats from transnational extremist groups and the potential for large‑scale civil unrest. Coordination will involve multiple agencies, including the Metropolitan Police, MI5, and international partners, while local governance will require robust public‑private partnerships to manage crowd control, traffic disruption, and emergency response protocols.

## Key facts

- The G20 summit will be held on 27‑28 November 2027 at Manchester Central Convention Centre.
- Prime Minister Andy Burnham, a former mayor of Greater Manchester, will host US President Donald Trump.
- Manchester becomes the first city outside London to host a G20 summit.
- The UK assumed the G20 presidency in 2026; the current summit is in Miami.
- Security costs are projected to exceed £10 million, surpassing the 2009 London estimate.

## Implications

- Enhanced global profile for Manchester and the North of England, potentially attracting foreign investment and talent.
- A diplomatic reset in US‑UK relations, offering a platform for dialogue on trade, climate, and AI policy.
- Significant logistical and security challenges that will test the UK’s counter‑terrorism and event‑management capabilities.

## Outlook

If the summit proceeds smoothly, it could cement Manchester’s status as a permanent venue for high‑level international gatherings, reinforcing the UK’s commitment to decentralised governance. Conversely, any security breach or logistical failure could spark domestic political fallout and undermine confidence in the government’s ability to manage large‑scale events. The outcomes will also influence the UK’s negotiating position in upcoming G20 agendas, particularly on AI regulation and climate financing.

# Pope praises young people's 'energy and commitment' at huge open-air Mass in Paris

> [!summary] TL;DR — Pope Leo XIV celebrated an open‑air Mass for an estimated 700,000 people on the Place de la Concorde, lauding the enthusiasm of young Catholics while warning about AI’s threat to humanity. The event highlighted France’s complex church‑state dynamics and drew attendance from senior politicians, including Brigitte Macron and several presidential hopefuls.

## Background

The pontiff’s four‑day visit to France marks a renewed effort by the Catholic Church to reconnect with younger generations amid declining attendance across Europe. Recent surveys show a modest uptick in religious participation among 18‑35‑year‑olds in France, prompting the Vatican to emphasize youth engagement. Simultaneously, European policymakers are intensifying debates over artificial‑intelligence regulation, data‑governance, and the ethical limits of agentic AI systems—issues that have risen to the top of the EU’s digital agenda and intersect with broader concerns about open‑source development and transparency.

## Youth as the New Apostolic Frontier

Pope Leo XIV’s address placed young people at the heart of the Church’s future, describing them as “today’s Church.” This framing reflects a strategic shift: rather than merely appealing to tradition, the Vatican is leveraging the energy and digital fluency of youth to revitalize congregations. The 80,000 young attendees at the Stade de France and the massive open‑air gathering suggest a latent demand for spiritual experiences that blend liturgy with contemporary culture. Moreover, the Pope’s emphasis on “letting faith overflow” resonates with a generation that values authenticity and social impact, potentially opening avenues for faith‑based activism and community projects that could bridge secular‑religious divides.

## AI Warning in a Geopolitical Context

During the visit’s opening remarks at the Élysée Palace, Leo XIV cautioned against “losing our humanity amid a paradise of machines.” This admonition aligns with growing regulatory pressure across the EU, where frameworks like the AI Act are being refined to address agentic AI and autonomous decision‑making. The Pope’s warning also intersects with the editorial priorities of AI governance, echoing concerns raised by entities such as OpenAI, Gemini, and Anthropic about the societal impact of large language models. In a year marked by heightened debate over data‑privacy, algorithmic bias, and the role of open‑source models, the pontiff’s remarks serve as a moral anchor for policy discussions that balance innovation with ethical stewardship.

## Church‑State Dynamics and Political Repercussions

The Mass attracted a notable political cross‑section: French Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu, presidential candidates Marine Le Pen, Bruno Retailleau, Édouard Philippe, and Gabriel Attal, and President Macron’s wife Brigitte. President Macron deliberately stayed away to avoid violating France’s strict laïcité laws, yet his presence through family underscored the delicate choreography between religion and state. The event also highlighted the rising influence of youth‑oriented political movements, as candidates sought visibility among the 700,000-strong crowd. This convergence of religious and political spheres raises questions about the future of secular governance in France, especially as the EU pushes for greater transparency in political financing and campaign data usage—areas where AI‑driven analytics are becoming indispensable.

## Key facts

- Approximately 700,000 people attended the open‑air Mass on the Place de la Concorde, the largest public gathering of Pope Leo XIV’s French visit.
- The Pope highlighted the “enthusiasm, energy and commitment” of young Catholics, calling them “today’s Church.”
- He issued a warning about AI’s potential to erode humanity, referencing “a paradise of machines” during a speech at the Élysée Palace.
- French President Emmanuel Macron avoided the Mass to respect laïcité, but his wife Brigitte and Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu attended.
- Presidential hopefuls Marine Le Pen, Bruno Retailleau, Édouard Philippe, and Gabriel Attal were present, underscoring the political salience of the event.
- Earlier in the day, the Pope visited the Maison Bakhita centre, supporting migrants and refugees, and praised the “invisible” populations.
- The pontiff’s itinerary includes Lourdes, Metz (honoring Robert Schuman), and further engagements with youth and civil society.

## Implications

- The Vatican’s focus on youth may catalyze new forms of faith‑based community service and digital evangelization, potentially reshaping the Church’s outreach strategy across Europe.
- The Pope’s AI warning adds a moral dimension to ongoing EU regulatory debates, potentially influencing public opinion and legislative priorities for agentic AI and LLM oversight.
- The political attendance signals a pragmatic recalibration of secular boundaries, suggesting that future church‑state interactions in France may incorporate more nuanced engagement models.

## Outlook

Looking ahead, Pope Leo XIV’s French tour is likely to serve as a template for how religious institutions can engage with technologically savvy younger demographics while navigating complex geopolitical landscapes. The intersection of spiritual messaging and AI ethics will continue to shape policy dialogues, especially as the EU finalizes its AI Act and member states grapple with open‑source governance. In the political arena, the visibility of high‑profile candidates at religious events may prompt a re‑examination of campaign financing transparency and the role of data‑driven targeting in democratic processes. Ultimately, the gathering underscores a broader trend: the convergence of faith, technology, and politics in defining the social contract of the 21st century.

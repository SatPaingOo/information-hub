# OpenAI working on new $30B raise at $1.25T valuation ahead of IPO

> [!summary] TL;DR — OpenAI is close to sealing a $30 billion funding round that would value the firm at $1.25 trillion, positioning it as the world’s most valuable private AI company and setting a high bar for its upcoming IPO. The round, led by Coatue with UAE participation, may also bring additional compute capacity from the Emirates, while competitors like Anthropic and Google’s Gemini continue to eye multi‑trillion‑dollar public valuations.

## Background

After a March 2026 raise that posted a post‑money valuation of $852 billion, OpenAI has been preparing confidential IPO paperwork since June. The new round would eclipse that figure, breaking the record for a private tech firm and intensifying pressure on rival AI labs that are also courting massive valuations. Reports from CNBC and Bloomberg suggest the terms are still fluid, with the UAE’s involvement potentially tied to securing more training compute, a strategic move amid escalating AI infrastructure competition.

## Funding dynamics and valuation implications

A $30 billion infusion at a $1.25 trillion pre‑money valuation would more than double OpenAI’s previous private valuation and cement its status as the most valuable private AI company ever. This mega‑round underscores the willingness of sovereign wealth funds (notably UAE) and major venture investors (Coatue) to bet heavily on AI leadership, even as public markets remain volatile. The scale of the raise also signals that OpenAI is opting for a longer private runway to consolidate its infrastructure advantage before facing IPO scrutiny.

## Strategic IPO timing and competitive landscape

OpenAI’s confidential S‑1 filing, filed in June, will now be priced against an unprecedented $1.25 trillion benchmark. This high bar could pressure the company to demonstrate robust earnings growth and clear regulatory compliance before listing. Meanwhile, Anthropic is rumored to target a $1.5‑$2 trillion valuation for its own IPO, while Google’s Gemini models continue to advance, hinting at a broader ‘AI‑valuation arms race’ among the top labs.

## Regulatory and open‑source considerations

The involvement of a state‑backed investor like the UAE raises questions about data sovereignty and potential geopolitical strings attached to compute resources. Regulators in the US and EU are already scrutinizing large AI deals for antitrust and national‑security implications. Additionally, OpenAI’s commitment to open‑source research (e.g., releasing Whisper‑v3 under an MIT license) may be leveraged to satisfy regulators while still maintaining a tightly controlled commercial product suite.

## Key facts

- OpenAI is negotiating a $30 billion funding round at a $1.25 trillion pre‑money valuation.
- Lead investors include Coatue, with participation from UAE sovereign funds.
- UAE funding may be coupled with a deal to provide additional compute capacity for OpenAI.
- OpenAI’s last private raise (March 2026) valued the company at $852 billion post‑money.
- OpenAI filed confidential IPO paperwork in June 2026; Anthropic is also preparing an S‑1 with a $1.5‑$2 trillion target valuation.

## Implications

- Higher valuation could set a new ceiling for private AI firms, influencing future venture deals and M&A activity.
- The tie‑in with UAE compute may shift geopolitical dynamics of AI infrastructure and data governance.
- Regulatory bodies may increase oversight of mega‑AI financing rounds, especially those involving state investors.
- Competing labs (Anthropic, Google Gemini) may accelerate their own fundraising to match or exceed OpenAI’s valuation.

## Outlook

If the round closes at the rumored $1.25 trillion, OpenAI will enter its IPO phase with a valuation that dwarfs most tech peers, potentially rewarding early shareholders but also inviting heightened market scrutiny. The company’s ability to translate this capital into superior model performance and compute efficiency will be the key driver of long‑term shareholder value. Meanwhile, the broader AI ecosystem is likely to see a cascade of larger funding rounds and more aggressive public‑market valuation expectations, reshaping the competitive calculus for the next generation of AI products.

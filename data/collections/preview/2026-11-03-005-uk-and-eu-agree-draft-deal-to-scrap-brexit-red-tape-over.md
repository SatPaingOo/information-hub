# UK and EU agree draft deal to scrap Brexit red tape over food exports

> [!summary] TL;DR — The United Kingdom and the European Union have reached a draft sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) agreement that would eliminate most post‑Brexit checks on food and agricultural trade. The deal, expected to be finalised at a summit later this month, promises up to £5.1 bn of annual economic benefit for the UK and marks the most substantive step in the so‑called Brexit reset.

## Background

Since the UK left the EU’s single market in 2020, exporters have faced a complex web of health certificates, phytosanitary checks and border fees that have increased costs and delayed shipments. The friction has been especially acute for perishable goods such as fresh meat, shellfish and seed potatoes, prompting industry groups to lobby for a streamlined regime. The new SPS draft, roughly 200 pages, mirrors the EU’s existing standards and proposes a “dynamic alignment” whereby the UK automatically adopts future EU updates, reducing the need for separate negotiations. The agreement sits alongside parallel drafts on emissions trading, youth mobility and a joint statement for the upcoming UK‑EU summit, signalling a broader attempt to reset bilateral relations after six years of post‑Brexit turbulence.

## Economic Impact and Competitive Positioning

The UK government’s estimate of £5.1 bn in annual gains stems from the removal of £200 Export Health Certificates, £25 phytosanitary certificates, and assorted inspector and sampling fees. For large agribusinesses, the cost savings translate into lower consumer prices and the ability to compete more effectively against EU producers in third‑party markets. Small‑scale farmers, who previously struggled with paperwork, will see faster market access, potentially revitalising rural economies in regions such as East Anglia and the Scottish Borders. However, the benefit hinges on the UK’s ability to maintain compliance with EU standards, a requirement that may limit future regulatory divergence, particularly in emerging areas like gene‑edited crops.

## Regulatory Sovereignty vs. Market Access

Dynamic alignment is a double‑edged sword. While it eliminates the need for duplicate regulatory bodies and offers certainty for exporters, it also curtails the UK’s freedom to set its own food safety and environmental rules. Critics from the pro‑Brexit camp argue that the deal undermines the principle of regulatory independence that underpinned the original 2020 trade agreement. Conversely, business groups such as the British Chamber of Commerce view alignment as a pragmatic compromise that safeguards trade flows while still allowing the UK to diverge in non‑critical areas. The tension between sovereignty and market access will likely shape future negotiations on topics like precision breeding and carbon border adjustments.

## Political Ramifications and the Brexit Reset Narrative

Prime Minister Rishi Sunak has framed the draft SPS deal as the cornerstone of a broader “Brexit reset,” using it to demonstrate tangible progress ahead of the UK‑EU summit. The move also serves domestic political purposes, offering a concrete win for a government that has faced criticism over lingering trade frictions. Within the Conservative Party, factions supportive of a full‑fledged re‑entry into the EU see the draft as a stepping stone, while hard‑line Brexiteers fear it may pave the way for deeper integration, including a potential customs union. The British Chamber of Commerce’s call for a systematic review of all relationship options—including a Swiss‑style arrangement—adds pressure on policymakers to articulate a clear long‑term strategy.

## Key facts

- Draft SPS agreement removes £200 Export Health Certificates and £25 phytosanitary certificates for UK‑EU food trade.
- Estimated £5.1 bn annual economic benefit for the UK.
- Dynamic alignment commits the UK to adopt future EU plant, animal and food standards.
- The deal will re‑enable exports of fresh sausages, burgers, shellfish and seed potatoes to the EU.
- Pet passports for UK animals travelling to the EU are included.

## Implications

- Reduced administrative burden could boost UK agricultural exports by up to 15% within two years.
- Dynamic alignment may limit the UK’s ability to pursue divergent agri‑food policies, especially around gene‑editing and sustainability standards.
- A successful SPS deal could create momentum for further integration on emissions trading and youth mobility, reshaping the overall UK‑EU relationship.
- Domestic political debate will intensify as the government balances trade gains against sovereignty concerns.

## Outlook

If Coreper approves the draft without major amendments, the SPS agreement is likely to be signed at the late‑November summit, unlocking immediate trade benefits. In the medium term, the dynamic alignment clause will require the UK to invest in regulatory monitoring capacity to keep pace with EU rule changes. The broader Brexit reset could evolve into a more integrated framework, especially if the UK opts for a customs union or Swiss‑style market access in future negotiations.

# Eiffel Tower shut by staff protest after female workers moved for religious visit

> [!summary] TL;DR — Staff at the Eiffel Tower staged a strike after female employees were asked to vacate their posts for a visiting Hindu religious delegation, prompting an investigation and political outcry over gender equality.

## Background

The incident unfolded on a Saturday when a delegation of roughly 100 members of the Bochasanwasi Akshar Purushottam Swaminarayan Sanstha (BAPS) visited the iconic Paris landmark. According to union representative Diane Davoine, women were instructed to leave their workstations so that male staff could be placed in their stead, ostensibly to limit interactions with female visitors. BAPS said the arrangement was coordinated with Eiffel Tower management to avoid disruption, while SETE (Société d'Exploitation de la tour Eiffel) later acknowledged that the conditions "should not have been accepted" and were inconsistent with its values of equality. The episode ignited a broader debate about France’s strict secularist tradition (laïcité), the rights of religious groups to organise gender‑segregated activities, and the dignity of workers in a high‑profile tourist venue. Political figures ranging from Paris Mayor Emmanuel Grégoire to far‑right Marine Le Pen and former Prime Minister Gabriel Attal condemned the treatment of women, signalling potential legislative attention to prevent similar occurrences.

## Gender Equality and Secularism Clash at a National Icon

The request by BAPS to limit interactions with women touches on a sensitive intersection of religious freedom and French secular law. While the French constitution guarantees freedom of worship, it also enshrines the principle of gender equality, which public institutions and private employers must uphold. The Eiffel Tower, as a state‑operated monument, is bound by republican values that reject any form of gender segregation in public service. The incident raises questions about how far accommodations for religious practices can go without infringing on fundamental rights, especially when the accommodation involves directing employees to step aside based on gender.

## Labor Relations and Workplace Dignity

The staff union’s swift response—organising a strike the following Monday—demonstrates the potency of collective action in defending workplace dignity. Management’s apology, issued after the protest began, suggests an acknowledgment of procedural failure. However, the escalation to a full closure highlights the fragility of tourism operations when employee morale is compromised. The strike also underscores a broader trend of workers asserting their rights in service sectors where cultural sensitivities intersect with commercial expectations.

## Political Fallout and Legislative Responses

Political leaders across the spectrum condemned the episode. Paris Mayor Emmanuel Grégoire announced an official investigation and pledged that equality would be enforced at all historic sites. Marine Le Pen warned that no cultural or religious practice could justify limiting women’s presence, while former Prime Minister Gabriel Attal declared that no belief could dictate women’s roles in French society. These statements indicate a possible push for clearer guidelines on religious visits to public monuments, potentially resulting in new regulations or stricter oversight by SETE.

## International Religious Freedom vs. Domestic Norms

BAPS, which opened its first major temple in France the day before the visit, framed the arrangement as a logistical measure to avoid inconvenience. The group’s emphasis on mutual respect contrasts with the perception of gender segregation as a violation of French norms. The episode will likely be watched by other nations balancing religious accommodation with secular standards, especially as France remains a reference point for laïcité in the European Union.

## Key facts

- The Eiffel Tower was closed on Monday after staff walked out in protest.
- A BAPS delegation of about 100 people visited the tower on Saturday.
- Female employees were asked to leave their posts to make way for male staff during the visit.
- SETE confirmed the delegation requested the arrangement to limit interactions with women and later apologised.
- Paris Mayor Emmanuel Grégoire announced an investigation into the circumstances.
- Political figures including Marine Le Pen and Gabriel Attal condemned the incident.

## Implications

- Potential disruption to tourism revenue and visitor experience at a major Parisian landmark.
- Increased scrutiny of how religious groups negotiate access to public sites, possibly leading to revised visitation protocols.
- Strengthened labour rights discourse, encouraging other service workers to defend gender‑based workplace dignity.
- Possible legislative or policy measures to reinforce gender‑equality safeguards in public‑service environments.

## Outlook

The investigation is expected to produce findings within weeks, which may result in formal disciplinary actions or policy reforms at SETE. In the short term, the Eiffel Tower’s reopening will depend on negotiations between management and the union, likely involving clearer anti‑discrimination guidelines for future religious visits. Long‑term, the episode could set a precedent for how France balances religious freedom with its unwavering commitment to gender equality, influencing both domestic policy and international perceptions of French secularism.

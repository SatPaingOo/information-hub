# Putin shows no sign of stopping the war as Russia doubles down on Ukraine

> [!summary] TL;DR — Vladimir Putin’s recent address to the State Duma and his speech at the Valdai Discussion Club signal a continued commitment to the full‑scale invasion of Ukraine, despite mounting battlefield losses and economic strain. The Kremlin’s narrative frames the conflict as a European war against Russia, while pursuing hybrid tactics aimed at eroding Western support for Kyiv.

## Background

The 2024 Russian parliamentary elections, dubbed the "Special Election Operation," yielded a United Russia majority unprecedented in size, granting President Vladimir Putin a political mandate to sustain his war policy. The State Duma’s composition now allows the Kremlin to claim overwhelming domestic approval, even as the war in Ukraine has inflicted heavy casualties, infrastructure damage, and economic sanctions. In the lead‑up to the Valdai Discussion Club, Putin’s rhetoric emphasized Russia’s confidence in achieving military victory, citing shortages of Ukrainian air‑defence missiles and the anticipated pressure of winter strikes. Concurrently, Moscow has intensified hybrid warfare against European states, deploying cyber‑attacks, sabotage, and drones that breach EU airspace, aiming to pressure the West into cutting aid to Kyiv.

## Domestic Political Consolidation

The 2024 elections served a dual purpose: legitimising the war effort and consolidating Putin’s domestic power base. United Russia’s record‑high seat count, achieved through a campaign framed as a defence of national sovereignty, provides the Kremlin with a veneer of popular support. This political cushion is crucial for sustaining the war budget and justifying continued sanctions‑heavy economic policies. By presenting the conflict as a patriotic duty, Putin seeks to quell dissent and maintain control over the narrative, especially in a media environment increasingly monitored by state apparatus. The State Duma’s composition also ensures smoother passage of emergency decrees and military funding, reinforcing the perception of a unified front against external threats.


## Military Strategy and Hybrid Warfare

Moscow’s strategy blends conventional offensives with hybrid tactics aimed at undermining European support for Ukraine. The Kremlin’s narrative frames the conflict as a European war against Russia, a framing that seeks to delegitimize Western aid. Hybrid operations—cyber‑attacks, sabotage, and drone incursions—have targeted European infrastructure and supply chains, creating a sense of vulnerability among NATO members. Lavrov’s statements at the UN General Assembly and his comments on “brute force” illustrate Russia’s attempt to rationalise its actions as defensive. Meanwhile, the Valdai speech highlighted expectations of a winter offensive to exploit Ukrainian air‑defence shortages, signalling a continued push for territorial gains despite battlefield setbacks.


## Geopolitical Repercussions and European Security

The Kremlin’s rhetoric and hybrid tactics have heightened tensions across Europe. Germany’s discovery of an explosive drone near Ukrainian cargo planes at Leipzig airport exemplifies the spill‑over risk. NATO’s response has been to increase deterrence posture, deploying additional air‑defence systems and reinforcing troop presence in Eastern Europe. The war’s economic toll on Russia, coupled with sanctions, has prompted a reevaluation of Russia’s strategic priorities, potentially leading to a shift towards asymmetric warfare. European states face a dilemma: sustain aid to Ukraine while managing domestic security concerns. The Kremlin’s narrative that Western support prolongs the war may influence public opinion in key European democracies, potentially affecting future policy decisions.


## Key facts

- United Russia now holds a record majority in the State Duma
- Putin’s Valdai speech reaffirmed confidence in a military victory
- Russia has intensified hybrid warfare against European states
- Ukraine faces shortages in air‑defence missiles
- Western aid to Ukraine is a focal point of Russian rhetoric

## Implications

- Potential for increased hybrid attacks on European infrastructure
- Risk of NATO escalation if Russia perceives a threat to its member states
- Domestic political pressure on European governments to reassess aid to Ukraine

## Outlook

The trajectory of the conflict appears to remain unchanged: Russia is poised to continue its full‑scale invasion while simultaneously employing hybrid tactics to erode Western support. The domestic political landscape in Russia will likely stay stable due to the United Russia majority, but the war’s economic strain may force future policy adjustments. In Europe, the balance between deterrence and diplomatic engagement will be tested as hybrid threats persist. The international community must monitor Russia’s hybrid operations closely and reinforce cyber‑defence capabilities to mitigate escalation risks.

# Iran's president tells Trump it will never 'bend the knee'

> [!summary] TL;DR — President Masoud Pezeshkian delivered a defiant address at the UN, vowing Iran will never submit to U.S. pressure despite Donald Trump’s threat to “annihilate” the Islamic Republic. He simultaneously opened the door to diplomacy, demanding an end to the U.S. blockade of the Strait of Hormuz and the release of frozen assets.

## Background

The United States and Iran have been locked in a low‑intensity conflict since February 2024, marked by coordinated U.S.–Israeli strikes, Iranian missile and drone attacks on Israel and Gulf states, and a series of tit‑for‑tat naval actions in the Strait of Hormuz. The strait, through which roughly one‑fifth of global oil and gas shipments pass, has become the focal point of economic pressure, with the U.S. imposing a blockade on Iranian ports and ships in April 2024. Earlier attempts at dialogue in June 2024 collapsed amid disputes over the blockade and Iranian demands for fee collection rights. The latest exchange follows a dramatic escalation: Trump’s UN speech warning of “annihilation” if a peace deal is not reached, and Pezeshkian’s retaliatory address, where he highlighted civilian casualties from recent strikes and called for a negotiated settlement that respects Iranian sovereignty.

## Diplomatic Calculus and Negotiation Dynamics

Pezeshkian’s speech reflects a calibrated blend of defiance and diplomatic overture. By publicly rejecting Trump’s annihilation threat, he satisfies domestic hard‑line expectations while simultaneously signaling willingness to negotiate. The inclusion of specific U.S. demands—ending the maritime blockade and unfreezing Iranian assets—provides concrete bargaining chips for any future talks. Moreover, the timing of his address, just before indirect talks brokered by Qatar between Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi and U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff, suggests an effort to shape the negotiating framework before direct discussions begin. The Iranian president’s emphasis on a “peaceful nuclear programme” and rejection of weapons development also aims to pre‑empt U.S. concerns, potentially easing a major point of contention in the broader security dialogue.

## Strategic Implications of the Strait of Hormuz

Control over the Strait of Hormuz remains the linchpin of regional energy security and a primary lever in the U.S.–Iran power struggle. Pezeshkian’s demand that “everyone benefits from the Strait of Hormuz while we are denied access” underscores Iran’s desire to retain sovereign authority, including the right to levy fees on vessels. The U.S. blockade, instituted in April 2024, has disrupted Iranian shipping and increased insurance costs for commercial traffic, prompting calls from India, Japan, and other major importers for unimpeded passage. Should a diplomatic solution be reached, the reopening of the strait could stabilize oil markets, reduce shipping premiums, and alleviate global energy price pressures. Conversely, failure to resolve the blockade could lead to further tit‑for‑tat naval incidents, risking a broader confrontation that might involve other Gulf actors or even escalate into a direct military clash.

## Domestic Politics and Public Narrative

Iran’s political landscape is split between reformist and hard‑line factions, and Pezeshkian’s UN performance is designed to appeal to both. By displaying photographs of the late Supreme Leader Ayatollah Khamenei and victims of alleged U.S.–Israeli strikes, he reinforces a narrative of resistance against foreign aggression, satisfying hard‑liners who oppose any concession. At the same time, his explicit openness to “dialogue and diplomacy without accepting the language of force” resonates with reformists and the broader populace weary of economic hardship caused by sanctions and blockade. The Iranian foreign ministry’s dismissal of the recent talks as “not anything new” further signals that Tehran intends to keep the U.S. engaged on its own terms, using the diplomatic process as a means to lift sanctions and regain economic autonomy while maintaining a stance of principled defiance.

## Key facts

- President Masoud Pezeshkian declared at the UN General Assembly that Iran will never “bend the knee” to U.S. pressure, despite Trump’s threat to “annihilate” the Islamic Republic.
- Pezeshkian linked any diplomatic breakthrough to the lifting of the U.S. maritime blockade of the Strait of Hormuz and the release of frozen Iranian assets.
- The U.S. delegation walked out of the chamber at the start of Pezeshkian’s address, reflecting the heightened diplomatic tension.
- Recent indirect talks between Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi and U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff, mediated by Qatar, are the first since June 2024 and are described as “constructive and promising” by Witkoff.
- Iran continues to assert its right to a civilian nuclear programme and explicitly rejects the development of nuclear weapons.
- A cargo ship attack in the Strait of Hormuz killed an Indian sailor, prompting condemnation from India’s foreign ministry and renewed calls for unimpeded passage.

## Implications

- A successful diplomatic resolution could reopen the Strait of Hormuz, stabilising global oil markets and easing energy price inflation.
- Failure to address the blockade may trigger further naval skirmishes, risking a broader regional conflict involving other Gulf states.
- Domestic Iranian politics will likely see hard‑liners reinforcing anti‑U.S. rhetoric, while reformists push for economic relief through negotiations.
- The U.S. may face increasing pressure from major importers to de‑escalate, as disruptions to shipping affect global supply chains.
- The nuclear issue remains a separate but critical track; Iran’s clear “no weapons” stance could facilitate confidence‑building measures if the U.S. offers sanctions relief.

## Outlook

The immediate outlook hinges on whether the indirect talks brokered by Qatar can translate Pezeshkian’s diplomatic overture into concrete agreements. While Trump’s rhetoric remains confrontational, the practical need to protect global shipping lanes and the economic costs of continued blockade suggest a pragmatic resolution is possible. However, deep‑seated mistrust, domestic political constraints on both sides, and the strategic importance of the Strait of Hormuz mean that any deal will be complex and likely require phased implementation, with verification mechanisms and incremental sanctions relief. Observers will watch for signs of movement on the blockade and asset unfreezing, as these are the most tangible levers for progress. In the longer term, a durable settlement could reshape U.S.–Iran relations, reduce the risk of accidental escalation, and restore a degree of normalcy to one of the world’s critical energy arteries.

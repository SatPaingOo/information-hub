# Nvidia says it won't reinstate Huang as it reports soaring sales

> [!summary] TL;DR — Nvidia posted record quarterly sales of $115.8bn and doubled its profit, while the board firmly rejected co‑founder Jensen Huang’s offer to return as CEO after his sudden departure. Interim CEO Colette Kress underscored continuity, halted Hopper shipments to China, and affirmed the company’s leadership stability amid rapid AI growth.

## Background

The AI chip market has become a cornerstone of global technology competition, with Nvidia dominating data‑center graphics processing units (GPUs). Recent months have seen heightened scrutiny of executive conduct, U.S.–China trade tensions, and debates over the strategic direction of AI infrastructure. Co‑founder Jensen Huang’s abrupt exit—triggered by allegations of personal misconduct and suspected espionage—created uncertainty about leadership continuity and the company’s geopolitical posture, especially regarding advanced processors like the Hopper H200 that are critical for both commercial and military AI applications.

## Leadership Transition and Corporate Governance

Nvidia’s board, chaired by Harvey Jones, opted to keep Jensen Huang out of the CEO role despite his public offer to return, signaling a decisive stance on governance after a high‑profile scandal. Interim CEO Colette Kress has moved quickly to reassure investors and partners, emphasizing that the company’s strategic priorities remain unchanged. The board’s refusal to reinstate Huang, coupled with a thorough internal investigation, reflects a broader trend among tech giants to enforce strict ethical standards and protect brand integrity. This approach also aims to mitigate any potential geopolitical fallout linked to alleged ties with foreign intelligence entities.

## Financial Performance and Market Impact

Nvidia’s latest earnings reveal a staggering surge: sales climbed to $115.8bn (up from roughly $57bn a year earlier) and net profit more than doubled to $65bn. The data‑center segment alone contributed $107bn, underscoring the relentless demand for AI‑focused silicon. Shares rose 4.8% in after‑hours trading, reflecting confidence that the company can sustain its growth trajectory even without Huang at the helm. Analysts note that the robust results counter concerns about a cooling AI hardware market, while also highlighting the company’s ability to generate cash flow that can fund R&D and strategic expansions.

## Geopolitical and Strategic Implications

The decision to suspend Hopper H200 shipments to China marks a notable shift in Nvidia’s export policy, likely driven by emerging U.S. export controls aimed at preventing advanced AI chips from bolstering Chinese military capabilities. This move could strain the Chinese market, which has been a key growth driver for Nvidia’s high‑margin products. Simultaneously, the company’s continued emphasis on AI leadership positions it at the center of national security debates, especially as governments worldwide race to secure AI infrastructure. The board’s careful executive search—balancing internal talent with external candidates—suggests an intent to navigate these complex geopolitical waters while preserving Nvidia’s technological edge.

## Key facts

- Quarterly sales reached $115.8bn, a doubling from the prior year.
- Net profit rose to $65bn, also more than double year‑over‑year.
- Data‑center sales contributed $107bn, accounting for the bulk of revenue.
- Board rejected co‑founder Jensen Huang’s offer to return as CEO.
- Interim CEO Colette Kress confirmed shipments of Hopper H200 GPUs to China are halted.
- Nvidia shares jumped 4.8% in after‑hours trading.

## Implications

- Leadership stability is reinforced, reducing short‑term investor anxiety.
- The pause on China shipments may affect Nvidia’s exposure to the Chinese market and could prompt diplomatic discussions.
- Strong earnings validate continued investment in AI infrastructure, supporting broader tech sector momentum.
- The board’s decisive governance actions set a precedent for handling executive misconduct in high‑stakes tech firms.

## Outlook

Nvidia projects another 13% revenue increase in the next quarter, indicating confidence in sustained AI demand. The company is actively conducting an executive search, aiming to appoint a permanent CEO who can balance innovation, regulatory compliance, and geopolitical sensitivities. Analysts will watch whether the new leadership can preserve the strategic advantages built under Huang’s tenure while navigating emerging export restrictions and competitive pressures from rivals such as AMD and Chinese chip makers. Overall, Nvidia’s financial health remains robust, positioning it to remain a pivotal player in the global AI ecosystem.

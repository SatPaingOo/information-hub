# Polanski defends leadership after Zionism row and by‑election loss

> [!summary] TL;DR — Green Party leader Zack Polanski has reaffirmed his position despite a controversial motion branding Zionism as racism and a defeat in the Holborn and St Pancras by‑election, prompting deep internal divisions and external criticism.

## Background

The dispute erupted at the Green Party’s annual conference in Brighton, where a motion declaring Zionism a form of racism was passed, becoming official policy under party rules. Polanski, who is Jewish, faced immediate backlash for not debating or voting on the motion, while former leader Caroline Lucas labelled it “dangerous” and “divisive.” The vote has split the party’s MPs, co‑deputy leaders, and membership, and has drawn condemnation from the European Green Party, Jewish leadership bodies, and Prime Minister Andy Burnham, who urged a reversal.

## Leadership Crisis and Internal Party Dynamics

Polanski’s response illustrates a classic leadership dilemma: balancing grassroots democracy with pragmatic electoral considerations. The Green Party’s rule that member‑supported motions become official policy forced Polanski into a corner where outright rejection could be seen as authoritarian, yet silence appears as capitulation. Senior figures such as Caroline Lucas have publicly questioned his authority, while four of five Green MPs opposed the motion, highlighting a rift between the newly enlarged membership base (now ~220,000) and the parliamentary wing. The party’s co‑deputy leaders are also split, suggesting that the leadership may need to re‑negotiate the motion’s status or risk further fragmentation.

## Impact on the Holborn and St Pancras By‑Election

The by‑election loss to Labour’s Sagal Abdi‑Wali, narrowing the Labour majority from 11,572 to 3,364, underscores how the Zionism controversy may have alienated centrist voters in an otherwise promising seat. Polanski’s campaign was overshadowed by daily media coverage of the motion, diverting attention from policy proposals and local issues. While the Greens reduced Labour’s majority, the result signals that the party’s base may have been demobilised by internal strife, and that the controversy could hinder future electoral gains in marginal constituencies where Jewish voters and pro‑Israel constituents are influential.

## Geopolitical and International Repercussions

The motion has reverberated beyond the UK, drawing condemnation from the European Green Party and British Jewish organisations, which view the language as conflating criticism of Israeli policy with anti‑Semitism. Prime Minister Andy Burnham’s call for a reversal and his warning that the motion effectively rules out a two‑state solution indicate broader diplomatic concerns, especially as the International Court of Justice continues to hear South Africa’s genocide case against Israel. The controversy also raises questions about the Green Party’s stance on international human‑rights frameworks and its ability to maintain a coherent foreign‑policy position while preserving internal cohesion.

## Key facts

- Zack Polanski, the Green Party leader, is Jewish and defended his leadership after a motion branding Zionism as racism passed at the party’s Brighton conference.
- The motion, which defines Zionism as supporting an ethnonationalist Jewish state and backs a single democratic Palestinian state, is now official Green Party policy under its rules.
- Four of the five Green MPs opposed the motion; the party’s co‑deputy leaders are divided, and the Welsh deputy leader resigned.
- The Holborn and St Pancras by‑election saw Polanski’s party cut Labour’s majority from 11,572 to 3,364 but ultimately lose to Labour’s Sagal Abdi‑Wali.
- Condemnation came from the European Green Party, the Jewish Leadership Council, the Board of Deputies of British Jews, and Prime Minister Andy Burnham, who urged the motion’s reversal.

## Implications

- Potential erosion of Green Party electoral prospects in marginal seats where the Zionism issue may alienate key voter blocs.
- Increased internal pressure on Polanski to either clarify the party’s stance or risk further leadership challenges from within the parliamentary group.
- Broader diplomatic friction for the UK, as the government’s alignment with European and Jewish bodies may strain relations with constituencies sympathetic to Palestinian causes.

## Outlook

The coming weeks will likely see a showdown over the motion’s status, with Polanski under pressure to reconcile the party’s democratic processes with electoral realities. If he can broker a compromise that satisfies both the grassroots and the parliamentary wing, the Greens may recover; otherwise, deeper splits could threaten the party’s viability in future elections and its standing within the broader European green movement.

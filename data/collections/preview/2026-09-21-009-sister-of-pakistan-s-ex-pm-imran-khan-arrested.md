# Sister of Pakistan's ex-PM Imran Khan arrested

> [!summary] TL;DR — Aleema Khanum, sister of former prime minister Imran Khan, was detained in Lahore ahead of nationwide PTI‑organised protests, with authorities accusing her of mobilising supporters via social media and public meetings. The arrest heightens Pakistan’s political volatility, raises civil‑liberty concerns, and could shape the scale and tone of the planned 27 September demonstrations.

## Background

Imran Khan, a former cricket star turned politician, was removed from office in a parliamentary vote of no‑confidence in April 2022 and subsequently arrested on a series of corruption and money‑laundering charges. Since his incarceration, Khan has become a rallying figure for the Pakistan Tehreek‑e‑Insaf (PTI), which has staged repeated mass protests demanding his release and broader political reforms. The government, led by Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, has responded with a mix of concessions and crackdowns, citing security concerns. In this fraught environment, Aleema Khanum, Khan’s outspoken sister, has emerged as a key mobiliser, using social‑media platforms and public gatherings to coordinate PTI supporters ahead of a coordinated nationwide march scheduled for 27 September. Her arrest on 22 September, ordered by Lahore’s deputy commissioner, is framed by officials as a pre‑emptive measure to prevent “law‑and‑order” disruptions, while PTI labels it an illegal detention aimed at silencing dissent.

## Legal and procedural dimensions

The arrest order cites Section 144 of the Pakistan Penal Code, which empowers authorities to prohibit assemblies deemed a threat to public order. Critics argue that the order bypasses standard judicial oversight, as the detention is authorized by an administrative official rather than a court. International human‑rights organisations, including Amnesty International, have warned that such executive‑driven detentions risk contravening Article 9 of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, which guarantees liberty of movement and protection against arbitrary arrest. Moreover, the 30‑day detention period exceeds the typical 15‑day limit for preventive custody, raising questions about proportionality and due‑process safeguards.

## Political ramifications for PTI and the Sharif government

Aleema Khanum’s detention is likely to galvanise PTI’s base, providing a fresh rallying point that can amplify anti‑government sentiment. Historically, the imprisonment of high‑profile relatives of political leaders in Pakistan has intensified protest cycles, as seen during Benazir Bhutto’s exile and Nawaz Sharif’s disqualification. The Sharif administration faces a strategic dilemma: a heavy‑handed approach could deter protest participation but also risk delegitimising the government domestically and abroad. Conversely, a softer stance might embolden PTI activists to press for larger, more confrontational demonstrations, potentially overwhelming security forces in Islamabad, where shipping containers have already been deployed as barricades.

## Regional security and diplomatic fallout

Pakistan’s internal unrest reverberates across South Asia, where neighbouring states monitor the situation for spill‑over risks. India’s Ministry of External Affairs has issued a travel advisory for its citizens, citing potential disruptions in major cities. Afghanistan’s Taliban‑run government, still seeking international legitimacy, may exploit the turmoil to press for concessions on border management. Additionally, the United States and the United Kingdom have expressed concern over the treatment of political detainees, linking aid disbursements to adherence to democratic norms. Any escalation into violent clashes could jeopardise ongoing economic projects, such as the China‑Pakistan Economic Corridor, by disrupting logistics and investor confidence.

## Key facts

- Aleema Khanum was arrested in Lahore on 22 September 2026 on a deputy‑commissioner order; detention period set at 30 days.
- Authorities allege she was mobilising PTI supporters through social media and public meetings.
- PTI has scheduled a coordinated nationwide protest for 27 September, with Islamabad already fortified by shipping containers.
- Imran Khan remains incarcerated on multiple corruption and state‑gift cases, which he describes as politically motivated.
- The interior minister warned that the protests could harm national stability and pledged to stop them by force if necessary.

## Implications

- Potential surge in PTI‑led protests could strain law‑enforcement resources and trigger clashes in major urban centres.
- International donors may reassess aid packages, conditioning future assistance on improvements in judicial independence and human‑rights compliance.
- The arrest could set a precedent for using administrative orders to curb political mobilisation, influencing future governance tactics in Pakistan and the wider region.

## Outlook

In the short term, Islamabad is likely to see heightened security measures and a possible clampdown on PTI activities ahead of the 27 September march. If the protests remain largely peaceful, the government may opt for a negotiated release of Aleema Khanum to defuse tensions. However, any escalation into violence could precipitate a broader crackdown, further polarising Pakistani society and inviting heightened scrutiny from the international community. The episode underscores the fragile balance between security imperatives and democratic freedoms in a country still grappling with deep‑seated political fissures.

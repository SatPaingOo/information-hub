# Trump says US will form 'AI Force' and appoint an artificial intelligence tsar

> [!summary] TL;DR — Former President Donald Trump announced plans to create an AI Force and appoint an AI tsar, pledging to accelerate U.S. AI development while dismissing safety concerns. The move signals a hard‑line stance against regulatory delays amid a global AI arms race, particularly with China.

## Background

In the wake of growing international debate over the safety and governance of large language models, Trump leveraged his social media platform to announce a new executive initiative. He framed AI as the next industrial revolution, estimating it could contribute up to 25% of U.S. GDP. The announcement came as the U.S. and China intensify competition over AI supremacy, with Beijing investing heavily in generative models and the U.S. industry grappling with calls for regulation from researchers and lawmakers.

## Strategic Imperatives

Trump’s rhetoric underscores a belief that AI is a national security asset. By creating an AI Force, the administration aims to centralize coordination across federal agencies, streamline talent acquisition, and accelerate deployment of AI in defense, intelligence, and economic sectors. The appointment of an AI tsar would grant a single point of accountability, potentially expediting policy decisions but also concentrating power in a role that could sidestep existing oversight mechanisms.

## Regulatory Landscape

The initiative arrives amid a polarized U.S. policy debate. While Trump dismisses safety concerns as a "hoax," industry leaders like Sam Altman and Dario Amodei have called for a slowdown and independent monitoring. Congressional proposals for AI regulation, including mandatory kill switches and data provenance standards, face opposition from the executive branch. The AI Force could either serve as a platform for implementing voluntary industry standards or become a vehicle for preempting stricter legislation.

## Competitive Dynamics

China’s aggressive AI strategy—highlighted by state‑backed firms such as Baidu and Tencent—poses a direct challenge to U.S. dominance. Trump’s vow to keep the U.S. ahead of China signals a shift toward a more confrontational stance, potentially prompting a global AI arms race. The move may also influence international partners, encouraging allies to align with U.S. policy or to pursue independent AI pathways, thereby reshaping the geopolitical AI landscape.

## Key facts

- Trump announced the creation of an AI Force and the appointment of an AI tsar via a social media post.
- He claims AI could account for up to 25% of U.S. GDP and is essential for maintaining a lead over China.
- The initiative will rely on existing criminal and civil justice systems to target bad actors.
- He dismissed safety concerns raised by researchers as a "hoax."
- Industry leaders such as OpenAI’s Sam Altman and Anthropic’s Dario Amodei have urged caution and regulatory oversight.

## Implications

- Potential acceleration of U.S. AI development may outpace safety safeguards, increasing the risk of misuse.

## Outlook

The AI Force could become a pivotal instrument in the U.S. AI strategy, but its effectiveness will hinge on balancing rapid innovation with robust governance. If the administration adopts a collaborative approach with industry and academia, it may set a precedent for responsible AI deployment. Conversely, a unilateral, top‑down model risks alienating stakeholders and provoking international backlash, especially from allies wary of U.S. dominance in AI policy. The next few months will be critical as the executive branch outlines the structure, funding, and oversight mechanisms for the AI Force and the AI tsar, and as Congress responds to the administration’s hard‑line stance.

# Escalation of Aerial Warfare in Rakhine: Junta Airstrikes Target Civilian Populations Amidst Arakan Army Gains

> [!summary] TL;DR — A Myanmar military airstrike in Thandwe Township, Rakhine State, killed 14 civilians, including 11 females, as the junta increasingly relies on airpower to counter Arakan Army (AA) territorial gains. The strikes highlight a strategic shift toward targeting non-combatant areas as ground forces face significant losses.

## Background

The conflict in Myanmar has entered a critical phase as ethnic armed organizations (EAOs), most notably the Arakan Army (AA), have successfully seized control of vast territories, including 14 of Rakhine State's 17 townships. As the military junta loses ground in traditional infantry engagements, it has pivoted toward heavy reliance on aerial bombardment to disrupt AA supply lines and demoralize local populations. This tactical shift has resulted in a surge of civilian casualties, with data indicating hundreds of deaths and injuries in AA-controlled zones throughout the first half of the year. The recent strike in Kyauk Gyi village, near Thandwe, underscores the vulnerability of civilian infrastructure, including religious sites and residential homes, to these indiscriminate strikes. This pattern of warfare is part of a broader pattern of attrition where the regime attempts to maintain control through technological superiority in the air, despite losing significant ground and manpower in the southern and western regions of the country.

## Asymmetric Warfare and the Airpower Pivot

The Myanmar military's increasing use of airstrikes in Thandwe and surrounding regions is a direct response to the tactical successes of the Arakan Army. As the AA expands its control into Ayeyarwady Region and secures strategic hubs in Rakhine, the junta's ground forces are suffering heavy casualties. Lacking the manpower to engage in high-intensity ground combat against the highly motivated AA, the regime is utilizing its remaining air assets to conduct 'corched earth' operations. This strategy aims to create buffer zones and deny the AA access to local resources and civilian support networks. However, this approach is increasingly failing to achieve strategic objectives, instead fueling local resentment and driving further recruitment for ethnic armed groups.

## Humanitarian Impact and Demographic Vulnerability

The demographic breakdown of the recent Thandwe strike—where 11 of the 14 victims were female—highlights a disturbing trend of civilian targeting. The strikes are not merely collateral damage; they are occurring in residential areas and near religious sites like Buddhist monasteries. This pattern suggests a psychological warfare component intended to destabilize the social fabric of AA-controlled areas. The displacement of residents in Thandwe and Ngapali indicates a growing humanitarian crisis, as populations are forced to flee their homes to avoid daily aerial bombardments. This displacement creates long-term economic instability and places immense pressure on the informal economies that sustain these regions.

## Geopolitical Implications and Regional Instability

The territorial shifts in Rakhine State have profound implications for regional stability. As the AA consolidates power, the traditional state-centric model of Myanmar is being replaced by a fragmented landscape of ethnic governance. The junta's inability to hold territory in Rakhine State threatens the stability of border regions and could lead to increased refugee flows into neighboring countries. Furthermore, the regime's reliance on airpower, while technically effective in short-term tactical engagements, is diplomatically isolating the country further, as international observers witness the systematic targeting of civilians in areas outside of active combat zones.

## Key facts

- 14 civilians killed (11 females, 3 males) in Thandwe Township airstrike.
- The Arakan Army (AA) currently controls 14 of 17 townships in Rakhine State.
- Airstrikes have targeted Ngapali beach resort and villages in Chin State.
- Junta airstrikes killed 21 civilians and injured 76 in July alone.

## Implications

- Increased civilian casualties will likely drive higher recruitment for the Arakan Army.
- The junta's reliance on airpower signals a lack of confidence in ground-based military operations.
- Widespread displacement in Rakhine and Chin States will exacerbate the ongoing humanitarian crisis.

## Outlook

The conflict is expected to intensify as the AA attempts to seize the remaining regime-held townships in Sittwe and Kyaukphyu. The junta is likely to continue its reliance on aerial strikes to compensate for ground losses, leading to a sustained period of high civilian casualties and regional instability.

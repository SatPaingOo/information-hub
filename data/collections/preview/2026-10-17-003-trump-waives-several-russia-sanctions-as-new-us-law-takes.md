# Trump waives several Russia sanctions as new US law takes effect

> [!summary] TL;DR — President Donald Trump has suspended multiple sanctions against Russia just as a new U.S. law—named for Senator Lindsey Graham—aims to punish Moscow for its war on Ukraine is due to take effect. Trump justified the move as protecting U.S. national interests, but the decision has provoked bipartisan criticism, alarmed allies, and intensified debate over American policy coherence and domestic political calculations ahead of the midterm elections.

## Background

The Graham‑named sanctions bill, passed by the U.S. Congress and signed into law by Trump in September, was slated to become operative on 18 October. It targets Russia’s economy by authorizing tariffs of up to 100 % on the top five purchasers of Russian oil and gas—principally China and India—and imposes sanctions on President Vladimir Putin, senior Russian officials, banks, and a “shadow fleet” of tankers used to evade existing restrictions. In the weeks leading up to the law’s effective date, Trump signaled a shift in policy, announcing plans to import Russian diesel to lower domestic energy prices before the November midterms. A bipartisan group of 20 lawmakers responded with a letter urging Trump to implement the Graham law “in its fullest extent and without delay,” warning that any waiver could undermine deterrence and peace‑talk leverage. The executive order issued on Friday night blocked numerous sanctions, citing U.S. national interests, and triggered a coordinated backlash from both Democrats and Republicans, as well as from U.S. allies concerned about bolstering Russia’s war effort.

## Legal and Executive Authority Implications

Trump’s use of presidential authority to waive congressionally mandated sanctions raises constitutional and statutory questions about the separation of powers. While the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) grants the president broad discretion to modify sanctions, the Graham law’s explicit intent to punish Russia for its aggression appears to limit that discretion. Legal scholars argue that the waivers may be vulnerable to judicial challenge, especially if they effectively nullify the core punitive mechanisms of the legislation. Moreover, the move sets a precedent where future congressional sanctions could be easily circumvented by executive action, potentially weakening the legislative branch’s foreign‑policy leverage.

## Geopolitical Repercussions and Allied Concerns

The suspension of sanctions has reverberated across the transatlantic alliance. European partners, including the United Kingdom and Germany, expressed disappointment, fearing that the waivers could indirectly support Russia’s war machine by easing financial pressure. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky condemned the decision as a “gift to Putin” and an “investment in a war that must be ended,” underscoring the diplomatic rift. Meanwhile, Russia’s strategic calculus may shift: the partial relief could be interpreted as a sign of U.S. internal division, potentially emboldening Moscow to maintain its current military posture while seeking further economic concessions from other global powers.

## Domestic Political Calculations

The timing of the waivers—just before the midterm elections—suggests a blend of economic populism and political strategy. By promising lower diesel prices, Trump aims to appeal to voters in energy‑dependent districts, a tactic that historically yields short‑term electoral gains. However, the bipartisan backlash indicates that foreign‑policy orthodoxy still commands cross‑party support, especially on issues perceived as national security threats. The political fallout could erode Trump’s standing among traditional Republican foreign‑policy hawks, while simultaneously energizing progressive critics who view the waivers as an abandonment of Ukraine. The net effect on the midterm outcomes remains uncertain, but the episode highlights the growing tension between domestic economic concerns and America’s role as a global security guarantor.

## Implications

- Potential weakening of U.S. deterrence posture toward Russia, possibly encouraging continued aggression in Ukraine.
- Increased strain on U.S. alliances, as European partners question the reliability of American sanctions policy.
- Domestic political polarization, with Republican hawks and Democratic internationalists united in criticism.
- Energy market volatility, as the diesel import plan may temporarily lower prices but could expose U.S. consumers to geopolitical risk.
- Legal uncertainty surrounding the scope of presidential waiver authority under IEEPA and future congressional sanctions.

## Outlook

The immediate fallout will likely center on diplomatic protests and congressional scrutiny, with calls for legislative measures to limit executive waiver powers. In the longer term, the episode may prompt a recalibration of U.S. sanctions strategy, balancing domestic economic pressures against the imperative of maintaining a united front against Russian aggression. The outcome of the midterm elections will influence how robustly the next Congress pursues enforcement of the Graham law, shaping the trajectory of U.S.-Russia relations and the prospects for a negotiated settlement in Ukraine.

# UK says 'vital services' to continue in East Jerusalem as Israel says most consulate diplomats leaving

> [!summary] TL;DR — The UK Foreign Office says the British consulate in East Jerusalem will remain operational as a "UK Mission" after Israel announced the withdrawal of most diplomats in retaliation for British sanctions on West Bank settlements. Both sides frame the move as temporary and stress the need to keep essential services for Palestinians.

## Background

The British Consulate in East Jerusalem, established in 1838, is the United Kingdom's diplomatic foothold for relations with the Palestinian Authority and civil society in the city, the West Bank and Gaza. In September 2024 the UK imposed sanctions on Israeli companies operating in settlements deemed illegal under international law, prompting Israel to announce a 30‑day deadline to close the consulate. The announcement came amid heightened tensions over settlement expansion, the upcoming Israeli elections, and broader EU‑UK debates on trade with the occupied territories. The UK maintains that East Jerusalem is occupied territory and that its diplomatic presence is essential for delivering humanitarian aid, consular assistance, and supporting peace‑building initiatives.

## Legal and diplomatic framing

Both governments are using legal language to justify their actions. Israel argues it has sovereign authority over the city and can regulate foreign missions, while the UK invokes the 1949 Fourth Geneva Convention and UN resolutions that label East Jerusalem as occupied. The UK’s insistence that the building will not close, but merely be rebranded as a "UK Mission," is a diplomatic workaround designed to avoid a de‑facto recognition of Israeli sovereignty while preserving a functional presence. This semantic battle mirrors previous disputes over embassies in Tel Aviv versus consulates in Jerusalem, and sets a precedent for how states may respond to punitive measures without fully severing ties.

## Strategic implications for UK‑Israel relations

The episode tests the resilience of the UK‑Israel strategic partnership, which includes intelligence sharing, defence contracts, and joint research in high‑tech sectors. While the UK has signalled willingness to take "reciprocal action," it has so far refrained from expelling Israeli diplomats, likely to avoid destabilising cooperation ahead of Israel's election cycle. The limited task force of seven diplomats signals a compromise that preserves core services—such as visa processing for Palestinians, humanitarian coordination, and monitoring of settlement activity—while limiting Israel’s leverage over the mission’s day‑to‑day operations.

## Impact on Palestinian civil society and service delivery

The consulate’s continued operation, even at reduced capacity, is critical for NGOs that rely on British funding channels, for Palestinians seeking travel documents, and for monitoring human‑rights violations. A full closure would have forced many NGOs to reroute aid through the UK embassy in Tel Aviv, a route fraught with bureaucratic delays and political sensitivities. By keeping a minimal staff on site, the UK mitigates the risk of a humanitarian vacuum, but the reduced footprint may hamper the depth of engagement, data collection, and rapid response capabilities, especially in the volatile security environment of East Jerusalem.

## Key facts

- UK sanctions on Israeli settlement enterprises were announced in September 2024
- Israel gave a 30‑day deadline for the consulate’s closure as retaliation
- Around 20 diplomats and the consul‑general were withdrawn for security reasons
- A seven‑person task force will remain to provide "vital services"
- The building will be rebranded as the "UK Mission, Jerusalem"
- Both sides claim the move is temporary pending security assurances

## Implications

- Bilateral friction may spill over into trade talks, especially concerning high‑tech collaboration and defence procurement
- The precedent of rebranding diplomatic premises could be used by other states facing similar disputes over contested territories
- Palestinian NGOs may experience reduced capacity for reporting human‑rights abuses, potentially affecting international advocacy efforts

## Outlook

In the short term, the UK is likely to maintain the seven‑person mission while seeking security guarantees from Israel. Diplomatic talks are expected to intensify ahead of Israel’s national elections in November, with the UK leveraging its position to extract concessions on staff safety and unhindered access to Palestinian communities. If a broader settlement‑related sanctions regime is expanded, Israel may revisit the consulate issue, potentially leading to a full closure and a diplomatic downgrade that could reverberate across EU‑UK coordination on the Israeli‑Palestinian conflict.

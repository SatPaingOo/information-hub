# Trump and Zelensky appear at loggerheads despite Oslo optimism

> [!summary] TL;DR — A brief hug in Oslo between US envoy Steve Witkoff and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky failed to mask deep mistrust. Trump’s recent public attacks and policy reversals have strained US‑Ukraine ties, raising doubts about continued support for Kyiv’s war effort.

## Background

The Ukrainian conflict has exposed a pattern of volatility in US policy under President Donald Trump. After a heated Oval Office confrontation in February 2025 that temporarily halted military aid, the administration has oscillated between support and skepticism. Recent actions—such as a diesel deal with Russia, the waiver of sanctions, and alleged intelligence sharing restrictions—have amplified Kyiv’s concerns that Washington’s commitment is unreliable. The Oslo meeting, intended to signal renewed cooperation, is now viewed as a diplomatic veneer over a fractured partnership.

## US Policy Inconsistency and Credibility

Trump’s rhetoric has shifted from hardline anti‑Russia to a pragmatic engagement that includes easing sanctions and negotiating fuel deals. This volatility erodes the credibility of US commitments, especially when policy changes occur in response to domestic politics rather than strategic calculus. The rapid reversal on Patriot missile transfers and the abrupt pause in intelligence sharing during critical missile attacks further undermine trust. For Kyiv, the perception that the US can pivot on its support at a moment’s notice threatens to destabilize its strategic planning and force a re‑evaluation of its reliance on American aid.

## Ukraine's Strategic Calculus and Perception of US Commitment

Zelensky’s public statements reflect a growing frustration with the unpredictability of US policy. The Ukrainian leader’s warning that “some political decision has been made to slow down support” signals a shift toward seeking alternative partners, including China and European allies. The limited availability of Patriot interceptors and the alleged restriction on Starlink access have tangible operational consequences, reducing Kyiv’s ability to counter Russian missile strikes. Consequently, Ukraine is exploring diversified supply chains for defense equipment and considering a more independent deterrence posture, which could alter the balance of power on the Eastern Front.

## Broader Implications for NATO and European Security

The strained US‑Ukraine relationship reverberates across NATO, as member states grapple with the reliability of American military support. European allies are forced to reassess their defense budgets and accelerate procurement of domestic air‑defence systems. The perception of a weakened US stance may embolden Russia to intensify its campaign, potentially destabilizing the broader European security architecture. Moreover, the diplomatic friction between Trump and Zelensky could influence NATO’s cohesion, as divergent views on engagement strategy emerge among member states.

## Key facts

- Trump publicly criticized Zelensky, calling him a "bad guy" and questioning his statements about a diesel deal with Russia.
- Ukraine’s Patriot missile stockpile has dwindled to only a few interceptors, with transfer approvals slowed by the Trump administration.
- The US waived sanctions on Russia just two days before they were set to take effect, signaling a shift in policy priorities.
- Starlink access for Ukrainian forces has reportedly been restricted, limiting real‑time intelligence during missile attacks.
- The Oslo meeting featured a symbolic hug between Steve Witkoff and Zelensky, but did not address underlying policy disagreements.

## Implications

- Erosion of US credibility may prompt Ukraine to diversify its defense partnerships, reducing reliance on American aid.
- NATO members may accelerate domestic procurement of air‑defence systems, increasing defense spending across Europe.
- Russia could exploit perceived US weakness to intensify military operations, raising the risk of escalation.
- The diplomatic rift may hinder future US‑Ukraine cooperation on peace negotiations and ceasefire enforcement.

## Outlook

While the Oslo meeting offered a symbolic gesture of goodwill, the underlying policy inconsistencies suggest a prolonged period of uncertainty in US‑Ukraine relations. Unless the Trump administration stabilizes its stance on sanctions, missile defense, and intelligence sharing, Kyiv may accelerate its pivot toward European and non‑Western partners. NATO’s cohesion will depend on member states’ ability to compensate for reduced US support, potentially reshaping the alliance’s strategic posture in the coming years.

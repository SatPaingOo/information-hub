# Why is Microsoft's outage lasting so long and will emails be lost?

> [!summary] TL;DR — Microsoft’s global outage, now over 20 hours, is a technical capacity‑upgrade failure that threatens email delivery and critical services. The incident highlights the fragility of cloud‑based communications and raises concerns about regulatory oversight of digital infrastructure.

## Background

On the evening of 13 Nov 2026, Microsoft reported a worldwide disruption of core 365 services—Outlook, Teams, Copilot and related AI tools. The failure, traced to a network capacity upgrade, has left schools, hospitals, courts and governments unable to send or receive email reliably. Microsoft has confirmed the issue is not a cyber‑attack, but a cascading server overload that is proving difficult to contain. The outage is already being described by industry veterans as the most disruptive business‑email failure in recent memory, with over 430 million users potentially affected.

## Technical Root Cause

Microsoft attributes the prolonged downtime to an earlier network‑capacity upgrade that overwhelmed the secure‑connection layer of its server cluster. According to Professor James Ferryman (University of Reading), this layer, which establishes SSL‑protected links, becomes a bottleneck when millions of devices attempt to reconnect simultaneously. The failure follows a classic “snowball” pattern: one server drops, traffic is rerouted to the next, and the surge of reconnection attempts compounds the load, creating a feedback loop that prevents a swift restart. The company’s statement that the underlying issue is “addressed” suggests a partial fix, but the residual load and the need for a phased re‑establishment of services explain the extended timeline.

## Operational Impact

The outage’s reach is broad and multi‑sector. Educational institutions report missing class communications, healthcare networks in Toronto and the UK face delayed patient correspondence, and judicial systems from Los Angeles to Texas warn of slowed case processing. Microsoft’s warning of “intermittent access issues” and a 48‑hour timeout window means many critical emails may be delayed or never reach their destination, jeopardising time‑sensitive workflows. The impact is amplified by the reliance on Microsoft 365 for verification in financial transactions, account recovery, and appointment scheduling, creating a ripple effect across finance, logistics and public services.

## Strategic Implications

From a policy perspective, the incident underscores the systemic risk posed by a single vendor’s cloud platform. Regulators are likely to revisit guidelines on service continuity, data‑resilience standards, and the need for redundancy in essential digital services. The editorial priorities—agentic AI, LLM, OpenAI, Gemini, Anthropic—highlight the added complexity when AI‑driven tools (Copilot, ChatGPT integrations) also depend on the same infrastructure. In Myanmar, where connectivity is already fragile, the outage has further strained civic communication channels. The event also fuels debate on open‑source alternatives and the case for regulated “digital essential services” akin to utilities, potentially prompting new legislation on service‑level agreements and outage‑reporting obligations.

## Key facts

- Outage began late Thursday, 13 Nov 2026, and persisted for over 20 hours.
- Microsoft cites a network capacity upgrade as the root cause, not a cyber‑attack.
- At least 74 countries reported connectivity problems; services include Outlook, Teams and Copilot.
- Microsoft extended email timeout to 48 hours to protect delayed messages.
- More than 430 million Microsoft 365 users are potentially affected worldwide.

## Implications

- Critical sectors (healthcare, education, justice) face operational delays and potential safety risks.
- Regulatory scrutiny on cloud‑service providers is likely to increase, prompting new resilience standards.
- Enterprises may accelerate diversification away from single‑vendor ecosystems or invest in redundant backup communications.

## Outlook

Recovery is progressing, with email queues reported as "significantly reduced" and approaching normal levels. However, intermittent issues are expected for several more days. Microsoft’s phased re‑launch strategy, combined with heightened regulatory attention, will shape how organisations mitigate future single‑point‑of‑failure risks. The incident also serves as a cautionary tale for the rapid adoption of AI services that rely on the same underlying infrastructure.
